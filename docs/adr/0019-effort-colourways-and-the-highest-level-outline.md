# Effort colourways and the highest-level outline

Accepted September 20, 2026. Zach reviewed two rounds of effort-control prototypes and two references, Codex's effort slider and Claude Code's effort picker, and asked for Codex's slider with a reaction of Sotto's own at a model's highest level; he then chose the Wash arrival, asked to see it in other colours, and asked for those colours to be a choice in Settings and for the composer to wear an outline in the chosen colour while a thread sits at its highest level. This decides where those colours come from, which is the one place the main window's colour does not come from the theme.

ADR-0011 says every colour the main window paints derives from the active theme's roles through `--tt-*` tokens, and `themeTokens.test.ts` enforces it. Four of the six effort colourways are colours no theme carries: Cyberpunk (magenta through violet to electric cyan), Rainbow (a spectrum), Aurora (mint through teal to violet) and Plasma (deep violet through hot pink to amber). The other two, Ember (the warning role's gold, the colour the control turned before this) and Theme accent (the theme's accent role), derive from roles as ADR-0011 asks.

## Decision

**The effort colourway is a setting, `effortColor`, with six values, and it is painted as an attribute, not a role.** `state/appearance.ts` writes `data-effort-color` on the window root beside `data-theme`, previews it through the same pending-edit path as the other appearance choices, and caches it for the first frame. `tokens.css` keys one block per colourway on that attribute. Each block sets three hues (`--tt-effort-a`, `--tt-effort-b`, `--tt-effort-c`), a border hue and an ink; the mode blocks shape those into the fill, the wash, the tinted word and the thumb. Ember and Theme accent set their hues from `--theme-warning` and `--theme-accent`; the other four set literal `oklch()` colours, each with a bright ink for dark rooms and a deep ink of the same hue for light, so the tinted word reads at 4.5:1 on the card and the chip in every built-in theme and both modes. `themeTokens.test.ts` checks that for every colourway.

**Feature CSS still reads only `--tt-*` tokens and never branches on the colourway.** A colourway that paints its word as a gradient (Rainbow) does so through two tokens, `--tt-effort-text-fill` and `--tt-effort-text-image`, that every colourway declares, so `effortPicker.css`, `threadChips.css` and `threads.css` carry one rule each. The colourway blocks are keyed on the bare attribute, not on `:root`, so a Settings swatch can wear a colourway the root does not; a custom property substitutes its `var()`s where it is declared, so the gradients are re-derived on any element carrying the attribute.

**The composer wears an outline while a thread sits at its highest level.** The effort chip marks itself `data-effort-top` when the saved level is the last the model reports, and both composers (`.thread-prompt` and the managed `.agent-composer`) read that mark with `:has()`: the border takes a 30% tint of the colourway and a thin ring with a brighter run travels round it every 4.2 seconds. Reaching the top from below plays the arrival once, a tide of the colourway through the card and the composer with the letters of the level taking the colour in turn, and both borders tint and fade. Reduced motion, by the app's setting or the system's, shows the settled state: the tint, a still ring at half strength, nothing running. Nothing announces itself unasked here because the user chose both the level and the colour; the outline says the thread is set to spend the most.

**The control itself is Codex's slider shape.** A compact card above the chip: the level word and one line that says what the level costs, `Default` when the model reports a default, a 22px pill track with the stops as dots and a 26px thumb, `Faster` and `More thorough` at the ends, and Ultrathink as a check row on Claude threads with an editable draft. Dragging previews without saving and is magnetic near a stop; release saves; the wheel over the track, arrows, Home, End and the digits 1..N step. Escape, Tab out and a pointer outside close it; there is no Done. The pixel furnace it replaces (`docs/plans/effort-furnace.md`) is gone with its painter and tokens.

## Considered Options

- **Deriving the four fixed palettes from theme roles.** Rotating the accent's hue or mixing the warning role cannot produce a magenta-to-cyan pair or a spectrum from a single theme; the colourways would have read as one more accent tint, which is the look ADR-0011 removed.
- **Making the colourway part of the theme file.** Every theme, built-in and imported, would have needed the fields, and a T3 Code theme has no place for them. The colourway is one choice across all themes, so it sits beside contrast and glass instead.
- **Branching feature CSS on `data-effort-color`.** Six rules per surface, and the `themeTokens.test.ts` philosophy broken for one control. Tokens that carry a gradient keep the branch in `tokens.css` where the palettes already are.
- **No outline; the tint on the chip alone.** The chip already tints. Zach asked for the composer to show the state the way Devin's input shows an agent working, and a state the user set twice over is not an announcement.

## Consequences

`effortColor` is on the settings patch allow-list and defaults to `ember`, so an upgraded settings file changes nothing. The Appearance page gains an **Effort color** subsection between Themes and the sliders, six swatches in the theme grid's columns, and the Live appearance card gains a sample of the composer at its highest level that plays the arrival when the pick changes. `README.md` and `CONTEXT.md` describe the control and the choice. The prototypes the pick was made from are `docs/prototypes/effort-slider-prototype.html` (the six colourways on the chosen slider) and `docs/prototypes/effort-color-settings-prototype.html` (the swatches and the sample on the Appearance page).
