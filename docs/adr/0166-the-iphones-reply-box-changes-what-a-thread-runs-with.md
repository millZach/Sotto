# The iPhone's reply box changes what a thread runs with

## Status

Accepted October 30, 2026, at Zach's request (#1922). It goes straight to stable and is not an Owl feature ([ADR-0072](0072-sotto-owl-is-a-windows-preview-release-track.md)). Zach picked direction B, "Chips in the box", from `docs/prototypes/iphone-thread-settings-prototype.html`. The research behind the three directions is `docs/research/2026-10-30-iphone-composer-thread-settings.md`.

## Context

The iPhone set a thread's model, effort and permissions only on New thread. Once the thread ran, its reply box changed none of them, and speed not at all, while the desktop's composer changes all four from its option chips ([ADR-0113](0113-the-composer-controls-are-text-buttons.md), [ADR-0143](0143-fast-mode-is-a-thread-setting.md), [ADR-0161](0161-the-quiet-composer-is-one-pill.md)). Zach asked for the iPhone's composer to change the model and effort, with Claude Code, Codex and T3 Code as the references, and confirmed it should cover everything a thread runs with: model, effort, speed and permissions.

The references put these in three ways. T3 Code's iPhone app has one chip that opens a Thread settings sheet holding all of them. Claude Code's desktop and web, and T3 Code's web app, have a chip for each under the prompt. Codex has one control for model, reasoning and speed, and permissions apart. The prototype drew one direction from each: A, one chip and one sheet; B, chips in the box; C, the desktop's split chip and shield.

The computer already took everything this needs. `configure-thread`, with `modelId`, `reasoningEffort`, `runtimeMode`, `providerMode`, `fastMode` and `speed`, has been on the remote allow-list since the desktop's own paired clients needed it, and a mode that lets a thread act without asking already needs the client's remote-answer policy (`remoteCommandNeedsAnswerPolicy`). Thread rows already carried the settings, the catalog each model's speeds, and the shell the changes a provider never confirmed.

## Decision

**1. The chips are the reply box's second line.** Under the words, inside the box: the model with its provider's logo, the effort in words, the speed bolt where the model offers more than Standard, and, at the line's end, the permissions shield. Each is a text button as on the desktop (ADR-0113), drawn 32 points tall and pressed across 44. The model and effort open an iOS menu, each effort level with its line about what it costs; the shield opens one with each permission mode and what the thread does under it. The bolt steps through the speeds on a press, an outline at Standard, one bolt at Fast and two at Ultra fast, told by shape. The shield is an outline while the thread asks before acting, half filled while it may act without asking, and filled for Full access. At the accessibility text sizes the model takes a line of its own above the others.

**2. It sends the desktop's change and follows the desktop's rules.** A press sends `configure-thread` with the one field it changes, the speed by name or, to a computer whose catalog names no speeds, as `fastMode`. What a press shows until the agent answers follows `pendingSettings.ts`: the press shows at once; one made while a save of the same setting is on its way waits, and only the newest goes; a permission change is a pending setting, marked on the shield with a line over the box saying what is still in force; a refusal puts the chip back and says why, with Try again; a change the agent never confirmed keeps its choice until the thread shows it or the computer lets it go. A change is never sent again on its own. What became of it is read from the thread the computer answers with, since the shell's error is shared.

**3. Only the thread's own provider's models.** The model menu lists that provider's ready models, as the desktop's does once a thread has started (#1601). Carrying on with another provider is a new thread, which the desktop begins ([ADR-0089](0089-a-thread-continues-on-another-provider-as-a-new-linked-thread.md)); the phone does not begin one.

**4. The chips wait when the agent would refuse.** As on the desktop, they are held while a turn runs or a prompt is still on its way to start one, while a request waits on the user, for an archived thread, while the computer or the agent cannot be reached, and while a permission change goes unconfirmed. Each chip's VoiceOver hint then says until when. Where the provider changes only a started thread's permissions (`configureThreadModel: false`), the model and effort are held and the shield is not. A computer that does not say it changes a thread's settings in place (`configureThread`) gets no chips.

**5. A grant is still the user's, and the computer's to check.** A mode that lets the thread act without asking can be chosen only where this iPhone may answer on that computer, as on New thread, and the computer checks the same policy again when the change arrives (ADR-0004). Choosing a mode is the user's own press, never something the phone does for them.

## Consequences

- The iPhone builds `configure-thread`, which it never did before. `RemoteCommands` lists it with the host's fields, and `Commands.configureThread` refuses anything else before it is sent.
- A model change the agent takes without the speed the thread had chosen says so on the bolt's line, as the desktop's `SPEED_NOT_CARRIED` does. Pressing the base model a thread already runs as its long-context variant leaves the thread on the variant.
- Not ported: favorites are this computer's window's own (ADR-0147) and the phone has none. The draft's suggested effort (ADR-0161 decision 3) and Claude Code's `ultrathink` are desktop only.
- The chips make the box one short line taller wherever a thread's computer offers them. The conversation makes room for it as it does for photos.
- The Focus journey `testAThreadsSettingsChangeFromItsReplyBox` (`--ui-thread-settings`) changes each setting on the simulator and keeps `thread-settings-*` captures in dark and light. CI runs it on the iPhone SE and the iPhone 16 Pro Max; the iPhone Duo is not among CI's simulators.

## October 31, 2026: the shield beside the bolt

Zach, testing build 45 on the unfolded iPhone Duo, asked for the shield to sit next to the bolt: at the line's end it stood far from the other three on a wide screen. The four now sit together in the desktop's order, the shield right after the bolt (or after the effort, where the model offers no speeds), and the room left over is after them. This replaces "at the line's end" in decision 1. Watcher's box takes the same line in a later change, below.

## October 31, 2026: Watcher's box takes the line

Zach asked for Watcher's box to match a thread's, with New Watcher as on the desktop (point 1 of the build 45 request, and his picks on the prototype: every provider in the model menu, another provider's model starting a new Watcher). Watcher's box now has the same line: model, effort, bolt and shield side by side, then **New Watcher** at the end, and a solid Stop the size of Send while Watcher's own turn runs. This reverses #1276's pick of a permissions chip in Watcher's hero: the shield opens the same sheet of four modes in Watcher's own words, and what is still in force goes over the box, as a thread's caption does.

The phone sends none of it as `configure-thread`: Watcher's thread is never one of the phone's own. A computer that offers `watcher-phone-composer` takes `watcher-phone-open`, the room's own Watcher open with the model, effort or speed (ADR-0073), which configures Watcher's thread for its own provider and replaces Watcher for another, as the desktop's chip does; `watcher-phone-new`, New Watcher; `watcher-phone-stop-turn`; and `watcher-phone-dismiss-handover`. Each names the current Watcher, and each keeps its outcome under its request ID as a management change does. A new Watcher keeps the saved permission mode, but the same mode lets another provider do more (Cursor edits in the project without asking where Codex only reads), so moving Watcher to another provider needs the phone's answers grant, as the shield's wider modes do: the computer refuses it otherwise, and the model menu lists only Watcher's own provider on a phone without the grant.

Not ported: the desktop also saves the **Orchestrating** role (#1041) when its chips change Watcher's model, effort or speed, so the next Watcher starts on it. The phone's change reaches the current Watcher, and New Watcher carries it, but the role is saved only from the desktop.

## November 1, 2026: one pill in Claude's row

Zach asked for the iPhone's reply box to look like Claude's iPhone composer, everywhere it appears: a thread's box (a new thread's included) and Watcher's, on the iPhone and the Duo, floating as before (#1946) and drawn in Liquid Glass under Glass (#1728). He picked variant A, "One menu", from `docs/prototypes/ios-claude-composer-prototype.html` (kept as the tag `prototype/ios-claude-composer`), over B, the pill with the bolt and shield beside it, and C, a sheet as in Claude.

This replaces decision 1 and the October 31 sections' layout. The box is one card with no edge in any state: the photos and files, the words, then a row inside it. On the left, the **+** and the **settings pill**: the provider's logo, the model, and the effort dimmer after it, with a small bolt while the speed isn't Standard, a small shield while the agent may act without asking, and a dot while a permission change waits on the agent. On the right, the mic ([ADR-0174](0174-the-iphone-dictates-on-the-device.md)) and Send, which is always there, lit once the box holds something; while a turn can be stopped, Stop takes Send's place until something is written, then stands beside it. At the accessibility text sizes the pill takes a line of its own above the buttons.

The pill opens one iOS menu: the provider's newest models (older ones under **Legacy**), then **Effort**, **Speed** and **Permissions** as submenus, each showing what it is set to. Speed is a choice in its submenu, Standard first, rather than a bolt stepped by presses. Watcher's menu lists every provider Watcher runs on, as before, and ends with **New Watcher**; its **Permissions** opens Watcher's own sheet of four modes. A computer that takes only Watcher's permissions from this iPhone keeps the shield alone in the row.

Decisions 2 to 5 stand. What decision 4 holds is held on the pill: the whole pill while a turn runs, a request waits or a permission change goes unconfirmed, with its VoiceOver hint saying why; only the model and effort in the menu where the provider changes only a started thread's permissions. Where Watcher's model, effort and speed are held, they are held in its menu with the reason at the top; while Watcher works, a computer that keeps a **next-turn choice** holds them for none of it ([ADR-0073](0073-watcher-coordinates-threads-with-its-providers-own-tools.md), November 1, 2026), and the pill shows the choice. Permissions and New Watcher keep their own rules. The pending permission no longer draws a dashed edge, since nothing in the box draws an edge; the dot and the line over the box remain.

The Focus journey `testAThreadsSettingsChangeFromItsReplyBox` now opens each setting from the pill and checks the row's order, and `testWatchersBoxChangesItsModelAndStartsANewWatcher` and `testWatchersOwnTurnStopsFromItsBox` reach New Watcher from Watcher's menu.

## November 1, 2026: the pill opens a sheet, providers closed

Zach found the pill's menu too long: every provider's models were listed open, so Watcher's ran several screens. He asked for the list to start at the top with the model in use and his favorites, then the providers, each closed until pressed and one open at a time, in every iPhone reply box: a thread's and Watcher's, on an iPhone and on the unfolded Duo. Of three variants in `docs/prototypes/ios-model-selector-prototype.html` (kept as the tag `prototype/ios-model-selector`), A folding providers open inside the menu, B the native menu with a submenu per provider, and C a sheet, he picked C.

This replaces the November 1 section's "one iOS menu". The pill opens the **settings sheet**, at its top and at half height, drawn up to full height by its grabber. In order: the model in use, ticked, with its provider's name under it; **Favorites**, the models starred on this iPhone that this sheet offers, in the order they were starred, the model in use left out; then one row per provider, closed, with its logo and how many models it holds. A press opens a provider in place and closes any other. Open, it lists its newest models, then **Legacy**, closed until pressed, holding the older ones ([ADR-0171](0171-claude-code-and-codex-menus-lead-with-the-newest-models.md)). Under the providers come Watcher's line on another provider's model, then the rows **Effort**, **Speed** and **Permissions**, each showing its value and opening its choices as an iOS menu, and for Watcher **New Watcher** last. Each time the sheet opens, every provider is closed, whatever was open when it was put away. Choosing a model or a setting puts it away, as the menu did, and so do **Done** and a swipe down; opening a provider or starring a model doesn't. Watcher's **Permissions** puts the sheet away and opens its own sheet of four modes.

A thread's sheet still offers only the thread's own provider, so its list is one closed row (Zach's answer), and its Favorites are only that provider's. Every model row ends in a star, which stars the model or takes its star off and chooses nothing; VoiceOver calls it **Add to favorites** or **Remove from favorites**. The stars are this iPhone's own: the desktop keeps its favorites in its window and never sends them, so Zach chose stars on the phone over carrying the desktop's (amending [ADR-0147](0147-favorite-models-lead-the-model-menu.md)). They live in the phone's preferences and reach no computer.

What the pill holds and when stands as the November 1 section says. Where the models are held, their rows wait in the sheet with the reason over them, and the providers still open and the stars still work.

The Focus journey `testTheSettingsSheetOpensAtTheTopWithEveryProviderClosed` checks the sheet's top, every provider closed, one open at a time, Legacy inside a provider, a star leading under Favorites, and the sheet opening closed and at its top again. `testAThreadsSettingsChangeFromItsReplyBox` and `testWatchersBoxChangesItsModelAndStartsANewWatcher` open a provider before choosing its model. SottoCore's `ModelSheetTests` cover the stars and the folds.
