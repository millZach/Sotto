# Glass is a see-through theme, held in Owl

## Status

Accepted October 26, 2026. Zach asked for "liquid glass" and picked Glass, see-through to the desktop, from `docs/prototypes/appearance-themes-prototype.html` and `docs/prototypes/appearance-prototype-kit.js` (`THEMES.glass`, `paintWindow` and `contrastReport`), kept at the tag `prototype/appearance-rework`. It is an Owl feature (`glass-theme`, [ADR-0072](0072-sotto-owl-is-a-windows-preview-release-track.md)). It amends [ADR-0084](0084-the-window-and-its-surfaces-are-always-solid.md), whose window is solid on every theme but this one, and [ADR-0011](0011-main-window-themes-replace-the-accent.md) and [ADR-0024](0024-sotto-ships-its-own-palettes-in-light-and-dark-columns.md), which gain a seventh built-in that only Owl offers. [ADR-0048](0048-the-main-window-can-let-the-desktop-through.md) recorded how the window was made see-through once before, for any theme; this brings its material back for one theme, whose colours were chosen for it. 0148 is claimed by an open branch, so this takes 0149.

## Context

A theme is colour roles, and every role reaches the page as canonical `oklch()`. The canonical form already carries alpha (`oklch(L C H / A)`), and the custom theme schema accepts it, but the window behind the page has always been solid black, so a translucent role only ever showed that black. The desktop can show through only where the operating system draws a material behind the window: Acrylic on Windows 11 22H2 and later, window vibrancy on macOS. Linux has no blur behind a window Sotto can rely on, and Windows 10 has no Acrylic.

The approved look carries several roles with alpha: the room at 80% by day and 76% by night, the sidebar, cards, the user's messages and the selected row over it. Many things read a role as a solid colour: the words' contrast tokens mix toward the canvas, a pill's ink is the canvas, the Sotto mark, the Dock and the widget paint from roles, xterm draws the terminal's, and a duplicate of a built-in copies its roles.

## Decision

1. **Roles stay solid; the see-through part is a set of tints.** Glass's 57 roles are opaque: the approved tints flattened over a quiet desktop (#e0e0e0 by day, #202020 by night, the prototype's glow), with every foreground set to read on them, so everything that reads a role keeps working. The translucent colours are separate: `GLASS_TINTS` in `src/shared/themes/glass.ts` (room, sidebar, raised, message and the three sidebar row states, plus the in-app backdrop's three stops). `appearance.ts` writes them on the root as `--theme-glass-*` only while Glass paints, and tokens.css puts them under the room, the sidebar and its rows, cards and the composer, and the user's messages, under `:root[data-glass]`. They are not roles and not in the theme file format, so no imported, custom or duplicated theme can carry them, and a duplicate of Glass is a solid theme. Alpha in roles stays as it was; it still only ever shows the solid window.
2. **The material.** Main decides once at start what it can draw (`glassMaterialFor`): Acrylic on Windows 11 build 22621 and later, vibrancy on macOS, nothing elsewhere. On macOS the material is `under-window`, the one AppKit draws behind a whole window's content, with `visualEffectState: 'followWindow'` so an inactive Glass window turns solid as every other Mac window does. The main window is made clear over its material when the saved look paints Glass in the half in force and the system does not ask for less transparency, and solid black otherwise. Picking or leaving Glass, or the half changing with the system's mode, switches the material in place (`setBackgroundMaterial`, `setVibrancy`, `setBackgroundColor`) through one main-window channel, `sotto:glass-window:set`. No restart and no new window are needed, so no copy says so.
3. **No flash.** The renderer paints Glass's own backdrop under the room (`data-glass="in-app"`) until main says the material is drawn, and only then lets the room go clear (`data-glass="see-through"`). Leaving Glass, the next theme paints solid at once and the material goes after it. A window made see-through tells its first frame so through a launch argument, so a saved Glass opens clear, and the material, not black, shows before the page paints.
4. **Fallbacks.** Where there is no material (Linux, Windows 10) or the system asks for less transparency, Glass is in-app glass: the same tints over a gradient in the theme's own hues (`--tt-glass-backdrop`), which is opaque. Windows' Transparency effects switch counts as such a system: Electron does not expose it, so main reads `EnableTransparency` under `HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Windows\CurrentVersion\Themes\Personalize` with Windows' own `reg.exe`, once before the window is made and again on `nativeTheme`'s `updated` event. While it is off the window stays solid and the room keeps the backdrop; when it changes while Glass paints, main tells the room first (`sotto:glass-window:changed`), so the room is opaque before the material goes. Windows still draws Acrylic's solid colour while the window is inactive; the tints keep their words readable over that as over any desktop.
5. **What stays solid.** Menus, dialogs, toasts and side sheets (`--tt-glass`, the overlay role), fields, code blocks, the terminal and its drawer, images and captures. The floating widget follows the system as before and wears Glass's solid roles. Watcher's composer keeps the solid card it measures its own frosted glass from (ADR-0126), so its words keep 4.5:1 over whatever passes under it.
6. **Readable over any wallpaper.** Every pair of words and surface the window paints (`tests/fixtures/renderer/paintedTextPairs.ts`) keeps 4.5:1 with the room's tint over pure white and over pure black behind the window, the worst any desktop can show before the blur softens it; the accent as a fill and the focus ring keep 3:1 on the room, the sidebar and a card. `tests/unit/renderer/glassTheme.test.ts` checks both halves over both, and the ordinary theme token tests hold Glass's solid roles to every check the other built-ins meet. That holds at every Contrast the slider offers, because Glass never paints its words softer than full strength: below 100% they would mix toward the solid room colour, which is not what is behind them when the desktop shows through, and at 50% a link on the selected row by day falls to 1.4:1 over black. `GLASS_CONTRAST_FLOOR` is 100, the slider's higher values still apply, and the Contrast slider says so while Glass paints. The lowest ratios: by day 5.86:1 over white and 4.60:1 over black; by night 4.83:1 over white and 6.36:1 over black; on the solid roles 5.64:1 and 6.36:1. To hold them, the status green deepens to `oklch(0.4 0.1 158)` on a Glass day, and links, warnings, errors and the primary action's hover are darker by day and lighter by night than the prototype's swatches suggested.
7. **The night scene.** It reads the surface it is drawn on to cap how strong a glyph under the words may be (ADR-0126). In-app glass paints its room as background images, so its tint is also the page's background colour, hidden under the opaque gradient, for the scene to measure. It now lays the backgrounds at and above Watcher's pane over one another down to the first solid one; under Glass's clear room none is solid, so the stack is laid over pure white and over pure black and the one nearer the words' colour is kept. That is the same whether the desktop or the in-app backdrop is behind, so the scene never repaints when the material arrives, and never blinks. The glow behind each word stays the canvas role, Glass's room over a quiet desktop.
8. **The Owl gate.** `glass-theme` is on the Owl list with `sentToStable: false`. Main reads it once at start: without it there is no material, no channel, no launch argument and no Glass in the Dock. The renderer offers Glass in Settings → Appearance and onboarding through `useOwlFeature('glass-theme')` and paints it only when main gave the window its Glass bridge, the same answer, there before the first frame. `BUILT_IN_THEMES` stays the six stable offers; `builtInThemesFor(true)` adds Glass, and resolution finds Glass only when the selection says the run has it. The id `glass` is reserved on every build; a theme of the user's own saved as `glass` before then is kept under the first free id after it (`glass-2`) and the halves that named it follow it, as ADR-0024 does for Graphite, Clay and Fern. A half saved on Glass, as an Owl install that shares its data with stable may save, is kept whole when settings are read anywhere and paints Sotto, and shows Sotto as chosen, where Glass is not offered.
9. **The iPhone.** It is not see-through and has no Owl gate of its own, so Glass is not offered there. Its palettes are generated from `BUILT_IN_THEMES`, which leaves Glass out (ADR-0051).
10. **Performance.** The blur is the operating system's. The renderer adds no `backdrop-filter`; the room's tint is painted once by the page, and the layers that would paint the canvas again stand clear (`seeThrough.css`). Main's per-update path is untouched: the material changes only when the window asks.

## Considered options

- **Alpha in Glass's roles, for built-ins only.** One source, but every reader of a role would have to learn alpha: the words' contrast mixes, the pill's ink, the mark, the Dock, the widget, xterm and the editor's duplicate. Rejected for the separate tints.
- **A see-through switch for any theme**, as ADR-0048 had. Rejected again: a theme's colours decide whether its words survive a white or black desktop, and only Glass's were chosen and tested for it.
- **Glass on the iPhone as its solid roles.** It would show as a plain blue-grey theme named for a look it cannot have. Left out.

## Consequences

- On this Windows 11 machine (build 26200, transparency effects on, energy saver off) Electron 43 drew Acrylic, and Mica and Tabbed as well, as their solid colour even in a focused probe window, and the taskbar showed no blur either, so the desktop was not seen through the window; `docs/verification/2026-10-26-glass-theme.md` has the captures. The in-app look was captured; macOS and Linux were not run.
- Known limitation: Electron cannot see whether a material was drawn, only ask for it. A compositor that paints the material flat although Transparency effects are on, as on the laptop this was checked on, and Windows' energy saver, which Electron does not expose, leave the window solid grey under a clear room instead of Glass's own backdrop. The words stay readable there, by the guarantee above, but the room looks flat until the system draws the material.
- Windows tints Acrylic by its own light or dark setting, as ADR-0048 noted; Sotto leaves `nativeTheme` alone (ADR-0009).
- The design captures paint `BUILT_IN_THEMES`, so they never show Glass, which depends on the desktop behind it. `tests/e2e/glass-window.spec.ts` captures it from the screen on Windows when `SOTTO_GLASS_EVIDENCE=1`.
- Sending Glass to stable is flipping its entry's `sentToStable`; nothing else in this decision changes.
