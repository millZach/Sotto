# The Threads sidebar opens on Needs you

## Status

Accepted October 24, 2026. Zach picked the combination of variants 3 and 7 from `docs/prototypes/thread-attention-prototype.html` (№11, with its overflow and all-caught-up states), after comparing ten ways a collapsed project folder could say that threads need him. His four calls, the same day: blocking requests lead, then oldest; no Undo; settled threads' requests surface, tagged; and it is an Owl feature first. The number is provisional until merge (see `docs/agents/domain.md`).

Amends ADR-0092 in one place: while this feature is on, the "N need attention" line under the search gives its place to this queue, and its Dismiss all to the queue's own dismissals. Nothing else in ADR-0092 changes: a failure is still seen by showing, by settling, or by dismissal, and requests are still never dismissed or counted as failures. Builds on the iPhone's Needs you (ADR-0051, CONTEXT.md) and changes nothing on the phones. ADR-0004 is untouched: the user presses every answer himself, and nothing here answers, approves or dismisses a request for him.

Amended October 27, 2026: every question its provider lets the user decline now has **Skip question** (or **Skip questions**) beside **Send answer**, on the card wherever it is drawn and on the sidebar's inline question, not only an optional one. Before, a required question, which is every Claude Code question, could only be answered. A skip is its own answer, `skipped: true` on the `answer` command, never an empty answer, and only a request the adapter marked `skippable` takes it; main refuses it on any other. A host that predates it marks nothing, so its cards offer no Skip and no client sends it one. Each provider is told in its own way, and the turn goes on: Claude Code's question tool is refused with "The user skipped this question. Proceed using your own judgement.", as its own client refuses a dismissed question; Cursor's is answered `skipped`; Codex's question is answered with no answers and its form declined; Devin's is declined; Muse's input is cancelled with that sentence as its reason, which Muse tells its model as a declined answer; and OpenCode's form is closed with that sentence, which OpenCode tells its model. Grok Build's question tool has only a cancel, which Sotto sends when a thread stops, and nothing shows the turn goes on after it, so a Grok question has no Skip. Claude Code's resume dialog has none either: it is a choice of how to go on, not a question. The card does not show a second Skip when its send button already skips, and a skipped question reads "Question skipped." on its card and "You skipped *thread*'s question." in Watcher's answered line. Permission cards keep Deny and gain nothing.

Amended October 29, 2026: an entry no longer draws its ask or answers in place. It says only what its thread needs, and one press pops the thread's card open over the sidebar (below, "October 29, 2026 amendment: the entry says what it needs").

## Context

A collapsed project folder said "● 4". The 4 was the folder's thread total; how many of them needed the user, and for what, was a bare 6px dot, with the count only in its tooltip and screen-reader text (`Indicators` in `ThreadSidebar.tsx`). Sighted users were told less than screen-reader users. The dot also lumped three different waits together: a permission, which holds the agent still; a question; and a failed turn nobody had seen, which blocks nothing and can be dismissed. A second count competed with it: the Dismiss all line, which counts unseen failures alone (ADR-0092). To learn anything, the user expanded folders one by one.

The iPhone already solved this shape. Its Threads page opens on **Needs you**: the threads' own questions and permissions first, settled threads included, counted on the tab, one-tap choices answered on the card. The desktop sidebar is the last surface still describing need with dots.

What the mock-up could not show decided the rest. A request is answered in its thread's pane and nowhere else, through `RequestAnswerStore`, which sends once, holds instead of resending when delivery is unconfirmed, and keeps drafts durable (`requestAnswers.ts`, `AgentRequestCard.tsx`). Requests carry no timestamp, so no client can honestly say how long one has waited. And no control is a pill (ADR-0110): the prototype's capsules and outlined amber box cannot ship as drawn.

## Decision

**The queue and its place.** While the Owl feature `needs-you-queue` is on, the Threads sidebar shows a **Needs you** card directly under the search field, where the Dismiss all line sits today (`SidebarFrame`'s `afterSearch` slot). "Inbox" is never said; CONTEXT.md already owns the words. The card lists one entry per thread that needs the user, across every project and host, and says how many in its heading. Project folders and the Settled shelf below keep their rows and totals; their collapsed dots are not drawn while the queue stands, because need is said in one place. Their accessible names keep saying it: a folder toggle still names how many of its threads wait on you. Searching does not change the queue, exactly as it never changed the Dismiss all line; the folders below still filter, and pending requests still stay listed there whatever is typed. The collapsed rail is unchanged; its rings already say need. At zero the card is absent entirely — no empty chrome, no "All caught up" line standing in the sidebar. While the feature is off, today's sidebar stands whole: dots, Dismiss all line, all of it.

**Who stands in it.** An entry stands for a sidebar row whose state is `needs` (`threadFacts.ts`): its own pending permission, its own pending question, or its own unseen failure. A delegated thread's wait rolls up as it does today (ADR-0081): the listed thread at the top of its delegation carries the entry, says `Waiting in <title>`, and offers opening alone, never an inline answer for another thread's request. Settled threads' permissions and questions stand in the queue, tagged **Settled**, as on the iPhone; their failures never do, because settling already saw them (ADR-0092, October 21 amendment). Closed and archived threads never stand. **Finished unread** and **Prototype ready** never stand and never enter the count: they keep saying so on their rows, softer and apart, as they do today. Watcher's Overview keeps counting a waiting prototype as needing the user (ADR-0108); the two surfaces already differ there and still do. One entry per thread: it stands for the thread's first pending request, permissions ahead of questions, as its row already picks. Answering it lets the next request take the entry's place from the next state.

**Order.** Blocking first, then oldest. Permissions, then questions, then unseen failures — the order `needRank` already keeps for the delegated roll-up — and inside each kind, the longest wait first, ties by thread ID. A failed turn blocks nothing, so no age of failure outranks a live agent held still.

**Waits are the host's to stamp.** A request's text says nothing about when it arrived, and a row's last activity is not the same fact. The host's coordinator therefore stamps each request when it first publishes it and each failure when it names it, and publishes both as add-only fields on protocol v1: `since` on a request, beside `failureId` for a failure. Stamps are kept in memory with the request and with `FailuresSeen`'s record, and a restart restamps what it still holds; a wait that survives a restart restarts its clock, which understates rather than invents. An older host publishes neither: its entries order by the row's last activity inside their kind, unknown activity last as everywhere, and show no wait words. Where a stamp exists the entry says the wait in the sidebar's own short form ("12m", "2h 05m"), and the heading names the oldest blocking wait when one is known. Wait words refresh when state changes, never on a timer of their own.

**Three stand, the rest are counted.** At most three entries show. The rest stand in one line under them: "+ N more waiting", with the kinds counted ("2 failed, 1 approval, 1 answer") and a **Show all** control that unfolds the card in place; Escape or the same control folds it back and returns focus to it. Resolving the top entry pulls the next in order up; the card keeps its height rather than jumping. The heading always counts everyone, folded or not.

**Answering in the queue is answering in the pane.** A queue entry never invents a second answer path. Where it offers an answer, it submits through the same `RequestAnswerStore`, with the same owner (`threadRequestOwner`, `requestAnswerOwnerKey`), the same `answer` command, the same Check again read (`observe-threads`), and the same words for its state: Sending…, Answer sent., the hold when arrival is unconfirmed, the error when main refused before dispatch, and `threadRequestBlocked`'s words while the thread's lane is busy or its provider is away. What main took is still noted for Watcher's lines where Watcher is on. The pane's card and the queue entry for one request share one entry in the store, so either surface sending closes the other. The comments that say a request is answered in its pane and nowhere else change to name the two surfaces and the one store. There is no Undo: an answer, once main takes it, is the user's answer, exactly as from the card. The prototype's Undo is rejected; a delayed send that could be cancelled would need a held state the store has never had, and a window closing inside the delay would have to choose the user's answer for him.

**Only what can be judged here is offered here.** Every other entry offers opening the thread, and says so plainly ("Open to answer"). Inline actions are offered only for the entry thread's own request, while its card could be answered: not busy, connected, no uncertain delivery, no answer already sending, sent or held, and no saved or half-written draft in progress.

- A permission offers its choices only when the whole ask fits the entry: the summary the card shows (`permissionSummary` — title, command, folder, details) within bounds the plan sets, at most three choices, none carrying a description, and no plan to accept (`context.plan` always opens). Legacy requests with no choices offered get Allow and Deny under the same rule. An explicit empty choice list keeps the card's words: answer it in the provider's app.
- A question offers its options only when it is one structured question, single-choice, with options and no free text, nothing unavailable, and no pages or missing link to show — a prototype question always opens, so its pages stand beside it (ADR-0085, ADR-0108). Pressing an option selects and sends through the store in one press; an optional question also offers Skip, as the card does. Legacy option lists and plain-text questions open, keeping their two steps and the composer.
- An unseen failure offers **Dismiss**, which sends the existing `dismiss-failures` with that one thread and the `failureId` its row showed. While two or more failures stand, the card's foot offers **Dismiss N failed**, the same command in one press, capped as today. A host that keeps its threads as they were answers as Dismiss all answers today, in the same words, and focus stays on the press; an accepted press moves focus to the next entry's first control, or to the search field when none is left.

**The look, translated.** The card is a filled card with its rows divided the way cards divide rows (ADR-0110). Its buttons are the app's buttons, its count a filled badge, its Settled mark a filled tag; nothing is a capsule and nothing is outlined in amber. Need is said as rows say it: the status ring, then the words — **Needs your approval**, **Needs your answer**, **Needs attention** — in the warning text token, with the wait and the project (and the host, where a row would name it) in muted text. Colour still comes only from theme roles through `--tt-*` tokens, text still meets 4.5:1, and the entry must fit the sidebar at its 260px narrowest and the window at 820×560.

**Keyboard and focus.** The card follows the search field in the Tab order, where Dismiss all stood. Its heading names the count. Every control's name says what a press does and in which thread, since choice labels repeat across entries. Nothing is announced: no live region, as the rows and the Dismiss all line announce nothing; alerts (ADR-0117) are unchanged. When an entry leaves, focus moves as dismissal above sets out; when a press is refused, focus stays where it was, beside the words that say why.

**Owl first.** The feature is listed in `src/shared/owlFeatures.ts` as `needs-you-queue`, `sentToStable: false`, owned by this ADR. The renderer asks `useOwlFeature('needs-you-queue')` before the queue exists. The host's stamps are not gated: they are add-only facts a stable desktop simply does not read. Sending it to stable later flips the one entry and retires the Dismiss all line and the folder dots for everyone in the same release; until then stable keeps both whole.

## Consequences

- The desktop gains a second place where a request can be answered. The store is what keeps that honest: one send, one hold, one draft per request, whichever surface the user pressed. Any future answer surface must go through it too.
- A thread now appears twice — in the queue and in its folder — as it already does on the iPhone. Its folder row keeps every action it has today; the queue entry opens it (beside, with the same chord rows answer to) and answers or dismisses only what is set out above.
- Settled is quieter than it was, and less quiet than its name promises: a settled thread's permission now stands at the top of the sidebar, tagged. That is the iPhone's rule arriving on the desktop, and the alternative — an agent held still where nobody looks — is what the tag costs.
- Waits understate after a host restart, and older hosts show none. Ordering inside a kind is therefore approximate across mixed hosts until hosts update; blocking still leads, which is the part that costs an agent its time.
- The design captures change wherever the sidebar head shows with the feature on, and are regenerated on purpose. CONTEXT.md's **Needs you** stops being an iPhone-only entry, and the guide's sidebar words follow.
- The phones, Watcher's room and Overview, the menu bar and alerts are untouched. No new setting, host, dependency or permission is introduced.

## October 29, 2026 amendment: the entry says what it needs

Zach's call, from a screenshot of an entry carrying a thirty-line command: the sidebar needs to say what a thread needs, not repeat what it asks. He picked variant B of `docs/prototypes/needs-you-trim-prototype.html` and the recommendation on each of its four questions.

- **The entry.** Its status ring, its title (a press opens the thread, as before), its wait, then one line: the row's own words for what it needs (**Needs your approval**, **Needs your answer**, **Needs attention**), **Waiting in** for a delegated wait, the **Settled** tag where it applies, and its press. The project and host words, the ask's text, a failure's reason and the inline choices are gone. Without the ask on screen there is nothing to judge an inline choice by, so "Only what can be judged here is offered here" above no longer offers any.
- **The press.** For the thread's own request, **Open to answer** pops the thread's card open over the sidebar, under its entry, without changing the thread on screen. Its name says what it opens: "Open to answer the approval in *title*", or "the question". The card holds the thread's title, what it needs and its wait, then the same request card as its pane: the same `AgentRequestCard`, store, owner, `answer` command and Check again, so the rule above that the two surfaces share one store entry now holds for the card itself. A free-text question's Write an answer opens the thread. **Open thread** opens it, Close or Escape closes the card and puts the focus back on its press, and a press outside closes it too. An entry for a thread's own failure keeps **Dismiss** and gains **Show why**, which pops the failure's reason with Open thread and Dismiss. A delegated wait still only opens the thread it is reached from. The card is a popover: the effort card's pattern, placed beside what popped it each frame it is open.
- **What the facts decide.** The entry's action no longer depends on the ask's shape or length: every own request is `answer` (pop its card), every own failure `dismiss`, every delegated wait `open`. The limits on command length, folder length, choice count and option count are gone with the inline answers.
- **The rail.** On Owl's rail (ADR-0146), while this feature is on, a waiting tile whose entry would pop a card pops the same card beside the rail, named as the entry's press is; every other tile opens its thread. The rail draws no text and no tray, so nothing else changes there.
- **Focus.** When an entry answered in its card leaves, the focus goes on as when any entry leaves, because the card is drawn inside its entry. When a tile's card closes because its thread stopped waiting, a focus the card held goes back to the tile.
