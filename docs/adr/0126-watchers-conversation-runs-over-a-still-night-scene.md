## Context

Watcher's night scene drew its sky and hills under the conversation's column at 35% by night and 20% by day, with a soft gap kept round each line of words. The gaps followed the words, so as the conversation scrolled the characters round every line shifted and distorted, which is what the user objected to. The column also read as a black panel, and Watcher's composer stood as a solid block over the foot of the scene.

## Decision

**1. The composer floats over the conversation, as a thread's does.** Watcher's pane is a thread pane and takes the same float rule (#1391); it did not float before because that rule was invalid CSS until the fix the slim composer builds on. The floating switch keeps its band below the pane, so the composer never covers it.

**2. The composer is frosted glass.** The card's colour at 72% or more, with `backdrop-filter: blur(14px) saturate(1.1)` and no edge (a faint hairline at rest until October 24, 2026, when the user asked for every edge round the composer to go, ADR-0110), so the scene and the words passing under it show through, blurred. A picture in the conversation can pass under it too, so the card is never thinner than lets every word on it keep 4.6:1 over pure white or pure black: the scene works that out from the theme's card and word colours and sets it on the pane. That is 80% in Sotto by night and 88 to 91% elsewhere among the built-in themes, and 92% until worked out. Asking the system for less transparency makes it solid. This is the one surface over the room that is not solid; the window and everything else stay as ADR-0084 has them.

**3. The scene runs under the column at 85%, by day and by night, and never moves for the words.** The per-line gaps are gone, and with them the scene's measuring of the conversation on every scroll and streamed word. The scene keeps nothing clear but the words that stand still (the header, what stands on the composer and the line under it) and runs on under the glass composer. Trees and the owls' perches stay in the margins. A ripple runs on under the column, and an owl that crosses it, flying between the trees, shows behind the words: both at the column's share and under its ink ceiling, as the hills are, so every word keeps 4.5:1 over them (amended October 25, 2026, pick A; the worst word is 5.0:1 by night and 4.8:1 by day in the prototype's measure). Over the margins the owls are drawn over everything, as before. Each word in the conversation carries a soft glow of the canvas, `0 0 3px`, `0 0 7px` and `0 0 12px` of `--tt-canvas`; what draws its own surface keeps its text plain.

**4. Words keep 4.5:1 without the glow, by an ink ceiling in the column.** A glyph's core is its ink at full strength, and at 85% the scene's ink would take the conversation's words well under 4.5:1 (at the strength the ground draws at, about 3:1 for the text colour and under 1.5:1 for its muted tier by night, and close to 1:1 by day, where the ink is made stronger). Rather than lower the share, the scene stops each ink in the column at the highest step at which every colour the conversation's words are written in, the text colour, its secondary tier and the link colour, at rest and pointed at, each laid over what is under it when a theme makes it translucent, keeps 4.6:1 (4.5:1 and a little for rounding) against the ink laid over the surface. The ceiling is worked out from the theme's own colours each time they change, so every theme, a saved one included, holds. In Watcher's conversation a link pointed at keeps the link colour and shows the pointer by its underline, since the theme's hover colour sits nearer the canvas and would hold the ceiling to about a tenth by day. One star is drawn to a cell, so no cell is painted twice past the ceiling. The share still decides which glyphs are drawn and how dense they read; the ceiling only flattens the darkest of them.

**5. The conversation's names and times are in the secondary text colour** (pick D2, October 24, 2026). In Watcher's conversation, everything the muted tier would write, the names and times over each message above all, takes the secondary tier instead. The muted tier sits only 6.3 to 9.5:1 on the canvas and held the ceiling to about a sixth to a quarter of the text colour by night and a tenth by day, so the column read faint; with the secondary tier the ceiling rises to a quarter to three tenths by night and about 28% by day. The rest of the room and the Threads page keep the muted tier.

**6. The scene never blinks** (the user's rule, October 24, 2026). Whatever changes in the room, a reply, a fold, the composer slimming or widening, Watched and back, the room's projection or the window's size, the scene keeps its last frame on screen until the next is drawn. Laying it out clears its canvases, so a layout draws again in the frame it is made; it holds its layout while the composer's card moves, keeps the full card's place while the composer is slim, and keeps its last frame while the room has no composer to set the column by. `tests/e2e/watcher-scene-steady.spec.ts` reads every frame the screen draws through those changes and fails on one where the scene's margin loses its glyphs.

**7. Watcher's owl flies on slow, deep beats** (amended October 27, 2026, Drake's pick E). The flight it had was one eased curve with a sine flap the whole way, a lean that followed its sideways speed, and its drawing turned into glyphs again at whatever fraction of a cell it had reached, with grid noise added, so nearly every glyph of it changed every frame (63 of 66 on a crossing) and its wing edges scattered stray dots. Now, in `planFlight` and `owlAt` (`nightScene.ts`):

- **Path.** It crouches for 0.16 s, drops a little off the branch, climbs into a high arc and comes in from a little under the perch it lands on, so the last climb does the braking. A crossing between the trees passes just under the moon. Its pace is 170 pixels a second, between 2.2 and 7.5 s in the air. One speed curve runs the whole flight: a push off the branch, a cruise, and a long braking approach that reaches the perch slowly and never passes it.
- **Wings.** A wingbeat is seven whole drawings, never a blend of two: wings high, two quick frames down, the bottom, and three slower frames up with the wing part-folded. The downstroke takes about a third of the beat. Two beats at 1.7 a second lift it off, a slower beat at 1.45 a second with the wings at their widest carries it across, two short beats at 2.2 bring it in, and then it flares, wings up and forward, legs reaching. On the perch the wings fold in three frames while the body settles with one small bob.
- **Cells.** It moves in whole cells measured from the perch it lands on, it leans only into turns, in steps of about three degrees, and its slow wobble moves it by a whole cell or not at all. Its drawing changes only where it beats or turns, never because it slid part of a cell. In flight its glyphs follow its ink alone: no grid noise, no lone faint cell at a wing's edge, and where a wing just was, one or two faint glyphs that fade within a few frames (`nightScenePainter.ts`).
- **Cadence and cost.** The scene keeps its 30 frames a second while the owl flies, and a frame whose picture would not change is not painted, so a crossing paints about 22 times a second against 26 before. A painted frame costs about 0.3 ms, as before. Measured on the prototype page in headless Chromium, the renderer ran at about 1.8% with the scene still and 6.1 to 6.5% while the owl flew a loop of crossings, against 7% for the flight it replaced; a loop of calm steps with motion reduced ran at 2.6 to 2.7%. 60 frames a second cost about 9.5% and was not taken.
- **Reduced motion.** Either the system's setting or Sotto's own turns it on. The owl sits as before, with no turning or blinking, and now and then still goes to another perch: it shows at two points along the same route for 0.7 s each, wings spread and still, then sits where it lands. There is no beat, bank or wobble, and the scene draws nothing between those steps; it wakes only for each. A capture's fixed clock (`held`) still keeps the owl at home and the scene unchanged.

## Consequences

- Measured on the built app at 1600x1000 and 1280x800 in all six built-in themes (`tests/e2e/watcher-column.spec.ts`), the lowest contrast of any word against any pixel the scene draws in the column is 4.87:1 by night (Tropic, which Fern replaced on October 26, 2026 under ADR-0024) and 4.82:1 by day (Sotto); the composer's words against the glass over the brightest of the scene, unblurred, 5.49:1 (Linen, by day), and over pure white or black, 4.63:1 (Linen, by day). `docs/verification/watcher-floating-composer.md` has every theme's numbers and ceilings.
- Sotto's own light palette writes links at about 7.2:1 on the canvas, below its secondary tier, so its links, not its names, set its ceiling by day (19% against the other themes' 28%). Lifting it would mean a stronger link colour, which is a palette change.
- The scene in the column still reads quieter than in the margins, as dotted hills and stars; the margins are unchanged.
- The scene no longer measures the conversation, so a scroll or a streamed word costs it nothing.
- Watcher's owl's flight changes far fewer glyphs from frame to frame (about 24 of 63 on a crossing, mostly its wings beating) and costs no more renderer time than the flight it replaced. `docs/verification/watcher-owl-flight.md` has the captures before and after, by night, by day, in Vellum and with motion reduced.
