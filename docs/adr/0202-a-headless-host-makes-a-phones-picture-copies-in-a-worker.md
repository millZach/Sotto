# A headless host makes a phone's picture copies in a worker, with decoders compiled into it

## Status

Accepted November 10, 2026, by Zach's pick on a question card: "Bundle a decoder in the host". **Number provisional until merge** (docs/agents/domain.md); 0201 is claimed by two open pull requests. Issue #2395, second part; the first is pull request #2422.

Amends [ADR-0170](0170-a-thread-shows-the-pictures-its-agent-made-as-tiles.md) decision 35, which said only the desktop makes the copy. Records the headless host's first compiled-in libraries, under [ADR-0018](0018-terminals-run-on-node-pty.md)'s rule: no production dependency is added. Adds no host feature: a headless host now lists `thread-media-edge`, which ADR-0170 added.

## Context

ADR-0170's November 10 amendment lets a phone's tile ask for a picture at its own size, and has the desktop answer a copy made with Electron's image tools. A headless host runs under plain Node, which reads no picture, so it listed nothing and its phones still fetched every picture whole. forge's phones are paired to its headless host, and the November 10 audit measured them: a median of 398 KB a tile where a 640-pixel copy is about 55 KB.

Three ways were open: a decoder compiled into the host's bundle, a native image library, or leaving those phones to fetch whole pictures. A native library needs a platform build and ABI story the host archive does not have; its verifier refuses any native binary. Zach chose the first, and accepted what it costs: a new library and its notices, about 76 ms of a worker's time the first time each picture is asked for, and scaling a little rougher than the desktop's.

## Decision

**1. A headless host makes the copy with pure JavaScript compiled into its bundle.** `jpeg-js` 0.4.4 decodes JPEGs and `pngjs` 7.0.0 decodes and writes PNGs. Both are devDependencies, compiled into the host's picture worker by `scripts/build-host.mjs`, never loaded from `node_modules` at run time: the host archive still ships `zod` alone beside its bundle. Only jpeg-js's decoder and pngjs's synchronous reader and writer are reached, so neither one's encoder nor stream code is in the bundle. Their licenses are in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`, which the archive ships. The archive's verifier now holds the bundle to exactly those two (`HOST_BUNDLED_PACKAGES`), where it held it to none, and a third is an ADR first, as a runtime dependency is. They were chosen as the plain, long-used pure-JavaScript codecs with no dependencies of their own; the other choices found were WebAssembly builds, which are not pure JavaScript, or little-used ports.

**2. The JPEG is written by a small writer of Sotto's own.** jpeg-js's encoder writes colour at full resolution, so its copies come out half as large again as one with colour at half resolution, as Electron writes, at the same quality: the test photo's copy was 46.6 KB as sent, more than a tenth of the picture, where Sotto's writer makes 29.3 KB. `src/host/jpegEncoder.ts` writes baseline JPEG from the standard's own tables with colour at half resolution each way, as Electron's writer does, at quality 85, the desktop's. jpeg-js reads back what it writes, as close to what it was given as libjpeg's own writer gets at 85.

**3. The work runs in one worker process, never on the host's own thread.** The host hands the worker the data URL it already read and takes back the copy (`src/host/pictureMaker.ts`, `pictureWorker.ts`). It is a process the host starts with Node's `fork`, not a worker thread as the store workers are: jpeg-js decodes into many small allocations, and on forge a worker thread's copies left the host's own process holding 154 to 182 MB more after the thread ended, where a process's left it within 17 MB, since a process gives its memory back when it ends. The worker makes one picture at a time, in the order asked; at most eight wait, and one more is sent whole at once. A picture and edge asked for again while its copy is being made waits for that one, for the desktop's copies too (`ReducedPictures`). The worker starts when a phone first asks and ends after 30 idle seconds, or with the host; one that does not answer within 15 seconds of the oldest picture waiting is stopped and its pictures sent whole; a picture asked for meanwhile does not start that clock again, only an answer does. After three workers in a row fail or go unanswered, the host makes no more copies until it restarts. A picture with more than 3840 by 2160 pixels' worth, a 4K screenshot, is sent whole, refused from its header before anything is decoded; the iPhone sends its photos at most 2576 pixels on the long side. A PNG is walked chunk by chunk first: one with a second header, which pngjs would read its size from, and an interlaced one, which pngjs inflates with no limit on what comes out, are sent whole.

**4. The copy follows the desktop's rules.** Fitted to the edge and rounded as the desktop's maker rounds it; a PNG when anything in the copy shows through, a JPEG otherwise; a GIF, a WebP, a JPEG carrying camera details and a picture already that small sent whole; and the copies kept in the thumbnails' 32 MiB, in bytes, never a count. Where it differs: Electron resizes with Skia's filter, and the host averages each box of pixels that lands on one pixel of the copy, which is softer on fine lines and text; neither library reads a colour profile, so a PNG or JPEG that carries one is sent whole rather than shown in other colours; and a JPEG jpeg-js will not decode whole, which Electron might show in part, is sent whole for the phone to show as it can.

**5. One path decides the maker.** The process owner hands the runtime its maker as `reducedPictures`: the desktop Electron's, a headless host its worker's. The host lists `thread-media-edge` wherever it has one. A headless host has one wherever its picture worker was built beside its `index.js`, on either release track; under the test runner, where none is built, it lists nothing and sends pictures whole, as before.

## Consequences

- A phone paired straight to a headless host is sent a tile's picture at a fifteenth to a twenty-fifth of its size for pictures made the size of forge's, about what a desktop sends: a 440 KB screenshot as 17 KB, a 445 KB photo as 29 KB (`docs/perf/2026-11-10-phone-picture-size.md`).
- The first picture after the worker has been idle waits for it to start, about 40 ms on forge; each picture then takes the worker 50 to 130 ms the first time it is asked for, decoding most of it, none of it on the host's thread. A copy stays kept until newer ones fill the budget.
- While it makes a copy the worker holds the picture and its working copies, about 90 MB at its peak for a 1920 by 1200 JPEG and 220 MB for a 4K one, all of it given back when it ends.
- The host bundle grows by about 100 KB, the worker's file. The host's external imports gain pngjs's unprefixed `zlib`, `util`, `assert` and `buffer`, all Node's own.
- Sotto now owns a JPEG writer. It is small and only writes, but a fault in it would send phones a picture that does not open; its tests read back every size it writes.
