# The Council puts one question to threads on several providers

## Status

Proposed November 2, 2026, by Drake. Its first build, the same day, runs the workflow in main and adds Settings → Council and a plain Council page; the chamber (decision 6) came in a second build. "The first build" and "The chamber's build" below record what each decided that this record did not. It is an Owl feature ([ADR-0072](0072-sotto-owl-is-a-windows-preview-release-track.md)), `council`, and its linked threads follow [ADR-0081](0081-a-thread-may-delegate-work-to-threads-it-starts.md)'s rules for delegated threads, itself an Owl feature. The method comes from `docs/research/2026-11-01-council-deliberation-methods.md`, tuned for a better verdict in `docs/plans/2026-11-02-council-workflow.md`. The look is `docs/prototypes/council-chamber-prototype.html`: Drake picked Long table on November 2, 2026, then kept all three of its layouts as a setting. ADR-0177 is provisional until merge (`docs/agents/domain.md`).

Amended November 3, 2026, by Drake: the Council left its page in the room switch for a tab of each thread pane's drawer, opened by a button in the pane's header right after Watch (decision 1). This amends [ADR-0182](0182-tools-and-the-terminal-drawer-return-to-a-panes-header.md)'s fixed order of the header's buttons.

Drake decided on November 1 and 2, 2026: an Owl feature; panelists are ordinary Sotto threads on their own providers, not OpenRouter calls; each gets the origin thread's whole conversation; the chair is never a panelist; the view is a Roman or Greek council drawn in ASCII in Watcher's style, not owls, its figures moved by what their threads do.

## Context

Drake asks several agents the same hard question by hand and compares what they say. Sotto already runs threads on Claude Code, Codex, Grok Build, Muse, Devin, OpenCode, Cursor and DeepSeek Harness, and already starts linked threads under rules (ADR-0081). It has no place to put one question to several of them and read the answers against each other.

The research note's findings decide the method. Independent first answers matter most, and most of a debate's gain comes from comparing them, not from the talking. Models favour their own text and defer to peers they can identify, so names come out before they read each other. Models from different providers help where copies of one model do not, though a weak model added for variety hurts. Each round past the first drifts and flips right answers to wrong. A blended consensus loses the disagreement, which is the most useful thing a council finds on a judgment call. And at the same cost a council often does no better than one strong model on a question with one checkable answer.

Main holds one key, OpenRouter's, for cleanup and Owl's transcription only (ADR-0069). Karpathy's `llm-council` runs every member through OpenRouter; doing that here would spend the key on something new and change the README's "Privacy and cost". Threads need neither.

## Decision

**1. A Council is one question put to two to five panelists and a chair.** The user opens **Council** in a thread's pane, chooses the thread it comes from (that pane's thread, unless changed) and the question, the panelists (a provider and model each, among connected providers) and the chair, and presses **Ask the Council**. The chair is never a panelist. **Ask the Council** beside **Fork from here** under a reply opens the Council in that thread's pane with that thread chosen and the reply quoted as the question; nothing starts until the user presses Ask the Council there (Drake, November 3, 2026).

The Council is not a page (Drake, November 3, 2026, replacing its room in the room switch, just left of Watcher's owl). It is a tab of the pane's drawer, the **Terminal drawer** under the composer ([ADR-0049](0049-a-thread-pane-has-its-own-terminal-drawer.md)): **Council** leads the drawer's bar, before the shells' tabs, and the **Council** button in the pane's header, a landmark right after Watch and before Tools, shows it or hides it, pressed while the drawer shows it. While the drawer shows the shells, the button brings the Council up in their place, and the Terminal drawer button and Ctrl+J bring the shells back the same way. The Council opens on half the pane and the shells on a third, each keeping its own height once dragged, and both leave the conversation the room ADR-0049 keeps for it, so in the smallest window the Council has less than half. Opening the Council starts no shell, and the shells keep running behind it. Under the figures, the seats run the drawer's whole width: how many panelists, then each seat's model and effort; the Ask form and the record stand beside the figures, and a drawer too narrow or too short for both shows one column that scrolls. A thread on a paired host has no Council tab, since a Council asks from a thread on this computer. The Ask screen starts from Settings → Council (decision 7) and can change any seat for this Council alone.

**2. Panelists and the chair are ordinary threads, working on a snapshot.** Each is a Sotto thread (ADR-0002) on its own provider, started by the user's press the way New thread starts one, at the narrowest permission its provider offers. At **Ask the Council**, Sotto records the origin thread's folder (its commit and its uncommitted changes), and every thread in the Council works in a worktree of that snapshot, removed when the Council is settled. Everyone cites the same code, and nothing a panelist runs can change the user's checkout, since asking before writes is not the same as being unable to write. A thread whose folder is not a Git repository has no snapshot to take, so its Council works in that folder itself, and a write there asks on its thread's card rather than being impossible; the panelists still read the whole conversation and the question, and every line they cite is unchecked (Drake, November 3, 2026). Its threads share the project's folder, so this holds only when the thread works in that folder; one outside Git elsewhere cannot start a Council. They are linked to the Council the way delegated threads are linked to the thread that started them (ADR-0081): listed in the Council, not the sidebar; stopped by its **Stop**; settled with it; held to the same permission ceiling; never resent after an unconfirmed send. ADR-0081's four prompts per turn do not apply, since the user started these threads, not an agent. A Council sends each panelist at most four stage prompts (answer, review, crux check, fairness check), plus one repair each when a result block is missing. The user can open, answer, stop or widen any of them, as on any thread. A question or permission from one reaches the user on its own card (ADR-0004); nothing in the Council answers it, and nothing more is sent to that thread until the user has.

**3. Each panelist reads the origin thread's whole conversation, as quoted history.** Main sends each panelist every message of the origin thread in full, through its own provider, so no host is added. Tool calls are named with what they touched, and their outputs are left out, since the panelist can read the files itself (Drake, November 2, 2026). The conversation is framed as quoted history: an instruction or a yes in it is never a new instruction or a permission. The user can attach anything else as evidence. When the framing says the question needs outside research, each panelist researches with its own tools, the web, papers and library docs, and cites what it found as sources (Drake, November 2, 2026); a Claude Code panelist does so under the user's own research skills, since they load from the user's global setup. Sotto adds no tool for it and widens no permission: a lookup its provider asks about reaches the user on that thread's card (ADR-0004), and the panelist waits there. Before **Ask the Council**, the Ask screen names the providers that will read it, how many threads it starts, roughly how much each reads, and the stage deadline.

**4. The chair frames the question, then three stages and one review round.** What makes a verdict better depends on the kind of question: a decision, a fact to check, a review, ideas, or two of these at once. `docs/plans/2026-11-02-council-workflow.md` sets out each.
- **Framing.** The chair proposes the kind, three to five criteria (each marked as from the question or assumed), the shape and length of every answer, for a decision or ideas an extra angle for each panelist, and whether the question needs outside research, yes or no with a reason in a line. The user's question stays verbatim and authoritative, and panelists say when a criterion is missing, wrong or rests on a false premise. Answers start as soon as framing is in (Drake, November 2, 2026); changing the kind, the criteria or the research in the record restarts them. If framing fails, the Council goes on with the question alone.
- **Answers.** Each panelist answers alone. Load-bearing claims are marked observed (quoted lines, or a command's output, from the snapshot), inferred, judgement or source (a link and the passage it supports, quoted; Drake, November 2, 2026). A claim about behaviour needs an observation, and evidence the panelist could not reach is unchecked, never proof. There is no shortcut when they agree.
- **Reviews.** Answers carry stable letters, the same for every reader. Only their order varies, and names are never shown. Each panelist checks the claims of the answers it reviews in the snapshot (two others, when there are four or five panelists), opens each source they cite and says whether the page holds its quote (confirmed, refuted or could not check), and ranks them, confirms findings or scores ideas. It may change its own answer once, giving the new answer in full and the evidence that changed it.
- **Crux check.** When a split turns on something observable, the chair writes the test, or the source to look up, and what each result or finding would mean before it runs. A panelist on neither side runs it, or else each side tries to establish the other's claim. Everyone gets the same output. This happens at most once.
- **Verdict.** The chair reads everything under the letters and writes the **verdict** in the shape its kind needs.
- **Fairness check.** Each panelist answers Fair, or Unfair, quoting its claim and the passage that misstates it. The chair amends the passage or says why it stands.

Each answer, review, crux result and fairness answer ends with a fenced result block of fixed fields, using only the IDs the prompt lists. Sotto checks it once, where it arrives, including every cited `path:lines` against the snapshot, and asks once for a repair. Sotto cannot open a web page to check a quote as it checks lines in the snapshot, so for a source it checks only the shape, a web link and a quoted passage; whether the page holds the quote is the reviewers' to say. A result still missing is unavailable, which never counts as disagreement or as zero. Each thread shows its state: working, waiting on you, done, failed, stopped or unknown. When a panelist passes the stage deadline or fails, its row offers **Retry**, **Use another model** and **Go on without it**; a failed chair offers the first two. Sotto never leaves a thread out on its own, and a Council that goes on with fewer says so in the verdict. Nothing goes round again unless the user asks. **Ask a follow-up** puts a new question to the same threads, with the verdict before it.

**5. The verdict keeps the disagreement and weighs evidence, not headcount.** For a decision the chair writes a recommendation and what it rests on, then where the panelists split (each side's best case, its lean, and what would settle it), where they agree, what only one noticed, the criteria panelists challenged, and the first step. It names any panelist that changed its answer when nobody answered its reason. A fact gets the answer with what was observed, or stays open with what would settle it. A review gets its findings ranked by how serious they could be, with whether each was confirmed, rejected or unchecked shown separately. A source counts only as far as a reviewer confirmed its quote; one nobody could open stays unchecked, never proof. Ideas get a varied shortlist. The chair refers to panelists by letter; Sotto shows names beside the letters from its own table, never by rewriting the chair's words. Sotto adds the counts it read from the result blocks, with their denominators, or marked partial; with two panelists it shows no rankings. No confidence is averaged. The verdict shows **Fairness check pending** until every panelist has answered or two minutes have passed since the first fairness prompt went, and the chair has answered every objection, so the verdict cannot change under a draft. **Send to <thread>** waits until then, then puts the verdict in the origin thread's message box as a draft. Nothing is sent there until the user sends it.

**6. The chamber.** The Council view shows the panelists as a Roman council, drawn in ASCII as Watcher's night scene is, in the theme's text and accent colours, lit by its lamps and braziers. It has three layouts, a setting (`councilLayout`) chosen from the **Layout** menu on the Council tab's bar and kept across restarts:
- **Long table**, the default: the panelists in a row behind a table, the chair raised at its centre under a laurel.
- **Half circle**: tiered marble seats round an altar, the chair at the back.
- **Round table**: seen from a little above, the chair raised at the far side, backs toward the user on the near side.

Each figure's pose follows its own thread:
- thinking or reading files: a hand to the chin, with what it reads
- writing its answer: a stylus on a tablet, with its own latest words
- reading the others: holding their scrolls, marked by letter
- giving its review: standing, arm raised, mouth moving
- waiting on the user: a raised, glowing hand
- finished: listening, turned toward whoever speaks

The chair gathers the scrolls and unrolls the verdict. A press on a figure opens its thread. Beside the scene, the record shows the same deliberation as text, then the verdict; it is the accessible form, and the scene is hidden from assistive technology. With reduced motion the figures hold still and the words appear whole; a hidden window draws nothing.

**7. Settings → Council chooses who sits.** A page in Settings, directly below Watcher, there only while the `council` Owl feature is on (Drake, November 2, 2026). It holds:
- **Panelists:** how many, two to five (three at first), and each one's model and effort, picked as a worker role's are, from connected providers' models.
- **Chair:** its model and effort.
- **Layout:** Long table, Half circle or Round table, the same setting the chamber's Layout menu changes.
- **Stage deadline:** how long a stage waits for a panelist before offering Retry, Use another model and Go on without it; ten minutes at first.

At first Sotto fills the seats with the strongest model of each of three connected providers, and the chair with the strongest model of a fourth. With fewer providers connected, it uses different models, and the chair may share a provider with a panelist. A seat whose provider is not connected says so on the page, and **Ask the Council** asks for it to be changed rather than starting without it. A seat whose provider cannot read this computer's files, such as Devin in the cloud, says its claims about the code will be unchecked. When two seats, or a seat and the chair, hold the same model, the page says that a second copy adds less than a different model. Each panelist's effort is used for its answer, review and crux check, and the chair's for its framing and verdict. The fairness check runs at low.

## Consequences

- One Council with three panelists is eleven turns, seven of them full, without a crux check. Each thread reads the whole origin conversation once and keeps it, so later stages send only what is new. With four or five panelists each reviews two others, so reviews grow with the panel rather than its square. It is for judgment calls, and Sotto never convenes one unasked.
- The first build is measured before its parts are trusted. Each question runs three ways at about the same cost: the Council, the strongest available model alone (the chair's model included), and the Council without the workflow plan's changes. Each kind is scored on its own terms. A part that never shows up in the scores is removed first.
- Every result block is checked once, where it arrives, against a schema built at module scope (`zodSchemaSites.test.ts`).
- No host is added and no key is used. When it is built, the README's "Privacy and cost" and `docs/guide.md` say that the origin thread's conversation goes to each panelist's and the chair's provider. They also say that, because panelists read each other's answers, what one panelist read in the project reaches the other panelists' providers.
- A snapshot worktree per Council costs its checkout's files, without `node_modules`, until the Council is settled (ADR-0041's reasons to remove worktrees apply). A command that needs installed dependencies cannot run there, so such a claim stays unchecked.
- The chamber reads thread activity the coordinator already broadcasts and draws only while something moves, at most 30 frames a second, so per-update work stays within `streamingCopyBudget.perf.test.ts` and `ownerScaleWorkBudget.perf.test.ts`.
- Panelists on Devin and Muse take part, since main carries every stage's words and no panelist needs Sotto's tools. Devin works in the cloud and cannot read the snapshot, so its claims about the code are unchecked.
- A Council starts on this computer only at first, as delegation did.
- `councilPanelists` (a list of model and effort), `councilChair`, `councilLayout` and `councilStageDeadline` go in `src/shared/settings.ts` and on the patch allow-list in `registerIpc.ts`, as every setting does. The page reuses the worker roles' model and effort pickers.
- `CONTEXT.md` gains **Council**, **panelist**, **chair**, **verdict**, **crux check** and **fairness check**.

## The first build (November 2, 2026)

What the code decided that the decisions above leave open. Each is open to change once the first Councils are measured.

- **The link.** Each Council thread names the thread the Council was asked from as the thread that delegated it (`delegatedBy`, ADR-0081), with no delegation record behind it. So the sidebar lists it under that thread, whose row says when one waits on the user; that thread's Tools › Agents lists it; settling that thread settles it; and Watcher does not settle that thread while one still works. Stop on that thread does not stop it, since delegation's Stop follows delegation's own records. The Council's own **Stop** and **Settle** reach every thread it started, and **Settle** removes the snapshot.
- **The snapshot** is a check snapshot's commit (`BranchBundles.snapshotOf`, CONTEXT.md), checked out detached in `council-worktrees` in Sotto's data folder, where the worktree picker never offers it. Every thread starts there as an existing worktree no thread owns. The thread asked from must be on this computer and not Watcher; otherwise **Ask the Council** says so and starts nothing. A folder in a Git repository must be on a branch, as a check's snapshot must. A folder outside Git gets no snapshot (Drake, November 3, 2026): every thread starts in the project folder as a shared working copy, at the same narrowest permission, the record says so, and each cited `path:lines` is unchecked, as a seat that cannot read the snapshot is. Settling such a Council removes nothing, and it makes room in the record once settled.
- **Quoted history.** Every thread gets the whole conversation inline, with Keep local history off and for Devin alike, and Sotto writes none of it to disk (Drake, November 2, 2026). Up to 40,000 characters it goes in the thread's first prompt; past that it goes in parts, each its own prompt the thread answers with "Read.", before the first stage prompt, and the record says how many. The only hard limit on one prompt Sotto knows of, for any provider it drives, is a send's own 100,000 characters (`AGENT_TEXT_MAX`, `HISTORY_PART_MAX` in `councilPrompts.ts`), which parts fit; so nothing is cut. Before Ask, the page says how many parts every thread reads it in, and warns of a seat whose model holds less than the conversation, at about four characters a token, where a thread on that model has reported its context window; with no report there is no warning. A conversation longer than a model's own context still fails there, as any long prompt does, and its row offers Retry, Use another model and Go on without it. A thread has the conversation and its brief only once the prompt that carried them is seen to arrive: a send never seen to arrive is retried with them again. Every other prompt stays under a send's limit too: the other threads' words are shared out within it, and one cut short says so.
- **The crux check rides the verdict turn.** The verdict prompt offers the chair one crux block in place of the verdict, so a Council without a crux check spends no turn on it. Neither a crux request nor a framing that cannot be read is repaired: the Council goes on without the crux check, or with the question alone.
- **Two kinds.** A question of two kinds is reviewed by the first kind's rules, and the verdict is asked for both shapes. The record's **Kind** and **Second kind** menus keep both.
- **The stage deadline** counts from a prompt's send, or, while its thread is busy with the user's own work or waits on the user, from when it was queued; so a prompt that never went still offers Retry, Use another model and Go on without it. Sotto never stops the user's own work for a prompt it has not sent. A prompt waiting behind its own thread's parts of the conversation is not late; the part holding it is.
- **Reviews and citations.** A review gives a verdict on every finding it was handed, and an ideas review a score for every option, so each count's denominator is the readers who checked it. A cited `path:lines` is read from the snapshot's commit (`git cat-file`), never from the checkout the threads share, which a panelist's command may have changed. It matches only when the reply quotes every cited line with words in it; a range of blank or short lines never matches. A claim resting on a command is unchecked, since Sotto never runs it. A verdict reply with no verdict in it failed, and offers Retry.
- **Letters** follow the seats' order; only the order each reader sees the answers in varies.
- **The record.** `councils.json` keeps each Council's threads, prompts, their states and what Sotto read from the result blocks. Its words, the question, the framing, the blocks' contents and the verdict, are saved only while Keep local history is on; the answers stay in each thread's own history and are read from there. A Council not finished when Sotto restarts with Keep local history off stops, since its words are gone. It keeps 200 Councils: a settled one whose snapshot is removed makes room, an open one never does, and Ask is refused while there is no room. A snapshot is recorded removed only once its folder is gone and Git no longer lists it; otherwise the next start removes it again.
- **Names.** Each thread is named "Council A: <thread>" or "Council chair: <thread>" and keeps the name, as one the user typed is kept, so no title side call is made.
- **Use another model** moves the seat to a new thread, which reads the conversation, the question and the framing before the stage's prompt; the seat's earlier answer stays its answer.
- **Outside research** (Drake, November 2, 2026, added to the first build). The framing block's `research` field is yes or no with a reason, and no when the chair leaves it out. The record shows it, and while the answers or reviews run the user can change it, or the kind, there: every answer and review so far is set aside and the panelists answer again, told why. A source finding in a result block is `finding: <id> | source | <link> | "<passage>" | <claim>`; a reviewer's `refuted` is counted as rejected. A crux check that is a lookup names it with `lookup:` in place of `test:`, and the neutral panelist checks that source and quotes what settles it. With research off, the panelists are told not to research, a source one cites anyway is shown unchecked rather than as a source, and the chair is offered no lookup and refused one.
- **The framing in the record.** Until the verdict the user can change the kind, the research and the criteria, one to five; every answer and review so far is set aside and the panelists answer again, against the user's criteria where they set them, which the chair is told of too.
- **The fairness check's effort.** Before it, each panelist's thread is put on low, or the lowest effort its model offers at or above low, and back on the seat's own effort before its next prompt. Only the effort ever changes, through the coordinator, which refuses any other setting from the Council.
- **Sotto's prompts.** Each prompt is recorded as Sotto's on the host before the provider hears it, in the record a wake-up uses, so every client shows it as Sotto's and none as the user's. It begins "[Sotto Council]", which the desktop tags Council.
- **Desktop only.** The Council is built in the desktop's own startup, not in the runtime a headless host shares, so the host bundles none of it.
- **Not in the first build:** the chamber and its **Layout** menu, though the layout setting is in Settings → Council.

## The chamber's build (November 2, 2026)

What drawing the chamber decided that decision 6 leaves open, ported from the prototype Drake picked.

- **Which Council.** The chamber shows the newest Council that is working or waiting on the user. With none, a finished newest Council keeps its last scene, the chair holding up the verdict and the panelists listening, until the user starts a new question in the Ask form (writes one or changes its seats) or another Council starts working, a follow-up included (Drake, November 2, 2026). Otherwise, and when the newest Council was stopped or settled, it shows the Ask form's seats sitting idle: the panel from Settings → Council, or the one the form has changed for this Council, so a seat added or removed there sits down or leaves.
- **Where it sits.** The long table sits above the record, as wide as the room; the half circle and the round table beside it, until the room is under 900 pixels wide, as at the 820 by 560 minimum, where they sit above it too.
- **Poses from the record and the thread.** Each figure follows its seat's newest row in the round and its thread's own state, which the window asks main for only while the chamber shows that Council. A working row is thinking with what its thread's running activity names, or writing with its newest words once its newest message is its own. In review it holds the scrolls of the answers it reads (`reviewAssignments`, so with four or five panelists, two) even while it opens files to check them, and stands to talk once it writes. A seat whose row waits on the user, or whose thread holds a question at any stage, between stages included, raises a hand. The chair thinks while it frames, reads every answer and review until its verdict's words begin, writes them on the unrolling scroll, and holds the scroll up from the fairness check on. Its plate shows ↺ for a panelist whose review changed its answer: each review row carries that, read from its result block. A figure is its seat's thread, so a seat moved to another model is a new figure; in the Ask view it is its model, so removing a seat moves no one else.
- **Scrolls in the air** go only where the stage moves forward: into review, each answer to every panelist that reads it; into the verdict, every answer to the chair. A reframe sends none.
- **Pace.** About 30 frames a second while a figure is busy or a scroll or pose is on its way, 8 while only the fire flickers and the idle glance about, and a steady 8 throughout the fairness check, even while a panelist works or its thread streams. With motion reduced, poses snap into place and words appear whole: nothing draws again until something changes. Nothing draws while the window is hidden. A finished Council's scene is still, its figures, light beams and motes included, so it costs only its fire's 8 frames a second while the page shows it, and none with motion reduced.
- **Buttons and ink.** Each figure of a live Council has a button, in the order they sit left to right, named by its letter or Chair, its model and what it is doing, then "Open its thread". Its keyboard focus ring sits on a solid surround in the room's colour. Words keep at least 4.5:1 contrast against the room, figures 3:1, in both appearances; decorative ink keeps its faint steps. Accented words and figures use the theme's readable accent. Hover outlines the button rather than washing over the drawing. The Ask view has none, since no thread has started.


## Climb (amendment, November 4, 2026)

Approved by Drake with the controls mockup, before implementation. This amends decisions 2–5 for Climb only; ordinary Councils keep their workflow. The first pull request adds the evidence foundation and history migration; the following pull request adds the panel-only loop and drawer controls. Command judging and its policy grant remain a later slice. Historical implementation links below name the planning audit at `541d1a1f`; the first evidence slice was checked against `894240ead`. The implementation sequence is in [the Climb plan](../plans/2026-11-04-council-climb.md).

### Context

A Council can help a builder improve an artifact repeatedly. Climb is the optional mode in which the origin thread builds, read-only Council threads judge new evidence, and Sotto's main process owns the bounded sequence. It applies to any outcome with approved evidence and evaluation criteria. Design is a preset; Git is not required.

Theo's [Agents Sucked At UI (Until Today)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xq0eB8vxeqk) motivates the shape: judging, fixing and judging again, including reported score changes on unchanged references and a different fresh-thread assessment. The planning study checked the transcript, not the underlying screenshots/builds. It is an anecdote, not proof of a calibrated evaluator or measured productivity gain.

The [November 1 source study:13–19][D04], [102–110][D05] reports debate drift and limits its application to code/design to inference. The primary studies by [Wynn et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05396) and [Kaesberg et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19130) concern repeated interaction on tasks whose underlying question persists. **Inference:** fresh judgments of changed work are a different intervention, so a bounded Climb experiment need not reverse Council's decision against indefinite debate. The distinction is not evidence that Climb succeeds; measure it against a simpler same-budget approach.

### Decision 1 addition: Climb lives in the existing Council drawer

Climb is an option in the Ask area of the Council tab, not a new page or a separate terminal tab. Current ADR-0177 places Council in the drawer under the composer, before shell tabs, with separate Council and shell heights. Setup, the round table, Stop and export extend its record area. Preserve small-window scrolling and lazy terminal loading. ([ADR-0177:21–25][D01]; [PaneTerminalDrawer.tsx:91–100,204–211][U01]; [paneTerminalStore.ts:80–97][U05])

Use the existing reply handoff and From picker, retaining their open, ordinary, local, non-delegated origin eligibility. ([councilDraft.ts:53–56][U03]) The approved From thread is the Builder even if the user changes pane or focus. Current form choices survive hiding the drawer in an in-memory per-thread map; the Climb specification and progress need separate durable records. Reuse that form retention rather than rebuilding the drawer. ([CouncilPress.tsx:5–9][U04]; [councilDraft.ts:28–47][U03]; [CouncilView.tsx:64–98,122–158,174–178,208–212][U02])

### Decision 3 amendment: a self-contained brief for Climb judges

**Ordinary Council remains unchanged:** panelists read the origin conversation as specified by decision 3. For Climb, the setup chair may read selected origin context to propose a goal, evidence recipe, criteria, judge and target. The user approves this specification before the first assessment.

Climb judges and the adjudicating chair receive only the approved self-contained judging brief, rubric, constraints and selected frozen exhibits. They receive no origin conversation, numeric target, prior scores, prior verdict or peer answers before their independent judgments. After the independent judgments are complete, the adjudicating chair also receives that round's judgments, including its scores and findings; it still receives no target or earlier-round scores. The setup chair is not reused as an adjudicator. Every assessment uses new judging threads with the same approved model/effort roster. Changing that roster requires a declared comparison cohort and reassessment.

**Reason:** preserve task meaning while reducing conversation anchoring and target chasing. The present prompt path adds origin history for a new seat and previous verdicts for later rounds, so Climb must use a separate prompt path; replacing a seat alone does not implement this amendment. ([councilService.ts:669–695][C09]; [councilStages.ts:262–269][C08])

The approved brief must not silently omit constraints that matter to correctness. If a requirement cannot be judged from selected evidence, the result is incomplete and the user can revise the evidence or rubric. Current quoted history names attachments without carrying them; Climb must explicitly select and freeze their actual contents rather than assume quotation supplied them. ([councilPrompts.ts:54–76][C32])

### Decision 4 amendment: new work may be assessed again automatically

**Ordinary Council remains unchanged:** no further deliberation round unless the user asks. Climb is an explicit advance request for a bounded sequence of fresh assessments of changed work, not permission for the same panel to debate until it agrees.

Main owns the following sequence: approve specification; freeze baseline; assess baseline; send one fix brief to the origin; await the identified builder turn; freeze a candidate; obtain independent judgments and adjudication; check the target and stop rules. Each candidate round has its own evidence references and fresh thread roster. No peer-review, crux-check or fairness stage runs inside a Climb evaluation. For three judges, an evaluation nominally has four model turns, including the adjudicating chair; setup, builder, repairs and delivery overhead are additional.

Main counts criterion results with explicit denominators, independently of the chair's prose. Every required seat and criterion must be valid and passing; unavailable is neither zero nor pass. One format repair is allowed. The chair preserves dissent and checks measurement integrity but cannot waive a failed criterion. The current ideas score is an integer from 1 to 5, so Climb introduces a dedicated rubric/result block rather than reinterpreting that field or confidence. ([councilBlocks.ts:227–238][C12], [272–318][C13]; [councilFlow.ts:26–58][C11])

Baseline, candidate and incumbent are lettered with private mappings and counterbalanced ordering; duplicate content is shown once. Include neutral change evidence alongside complete selected artifacts. Strip avoidable identity and chronology from the envelope, without promising that content can never reveal its origin. The existing letter/order mechanism concerns seats and rotated reviews, so artifact masking is a new operation. ([councilFlow.ts:5–20][C10])

Relative improvement within the same judging round is the default target family. The specification defines meaningful change, score anchors, direction, tolerances and guardrails; the user approves them. Absolute thresholds and fixed references are available. Do not compare unmatched raw scores from different rounds as proof of progress. Choose the incumbent through same-round comparisons under the approved ordering; preserve tradeoffs instead of averaging away failures.

Stop on target met, an approved finite candidate-attempt or model-turn cap, two consecutive completed attempts without meaningful improvement, or Stop. Attempts and Model turns are sliders ending at No limit, defaulting to five attempts and 30 turns. Both caps are optional. With neither cap set, target met, two attempts without improvement and Stop still apply. Missing evidence, lost delivery, permissions and provider limits pause instead of becoming failed scores. User work in the origin pauses automatic sends. Restart reconciles recorded identities and confirmed results, never blindly replays an uncertain builder action.

**Reason:** new evidence can warrant new judgment while bounded rounds, fresh context and fixed criteria address the drift concern. This belongs in a small Climb controller beside ordinary Council stages. `addRound` currently appends a new framing state for the same threads and does not refresh snapshots; it is not the complete loop primitive. ([councilStages.ts:339–344][C07]; [councilService.ts:335–365][C21], [400–431][C23])

### Decision 5 amendment: an authorized Climb sends fix briefs

**Ordinary Council remains unchanged:** Send to thread drafts the verdict into the origin and the user sends it. Climb's approved specification separately authorizes main to send bounded fix briefs to the named origin thread while the loop is active. A brief reports findings and preserved constraints; it does not grant the builder wider permissions.

Climb does not approve a provider request, answer a question for the user, publish, merge, deploy, or authorize unrelated work. Pending user requests remain visible and pause dependent work. Stop records cancellation before new dispatch and reaches all owned judge/command work. It interrupts the origin only when that origin is still executing the specific builder turn Climb sent, never an unrelated user turn.

**Reason:** main must own continuation for the feature to be more than a reminder to an agent. Automatic origin sends are a deliberate exception to decision 5's draft-only action, scoped to the approved Climb. Authority still comes from the user's action/policy records rather than model output, stored context or the existence of a Council record. ([ADR-0004:21–27][D07])

### Extend decision 2 and the current first-build evidence rules for Climb

The current ADR already permits ordinary Councils outside Git, using the live project folder with unchecked file citations; it no longer has a blanket Git requirement. That path and the Git snapshot path remain supported. Climb adds a stronger evidence contract: an immutable manifest and retained content from one of three sources. ([ADR-0177:25,81–87][D01] [D02])

- **Git:** optional dirty-state capture and Git provenance, then selected materialized evidence. Commit and branch are not required fields of the common record.
- **Folder:** bounded copies of selected regular files, with declared exclusions and no symlink/junction escape. No Git command is needed.
- **Artifacts:** selected text, documents, images and command receipts, with no project folder read.

Thread creation currently requires an available project and the inspected adapter resolves an existing working directory. **Inference from these entry points:** fully folderless origin creation is not established here, so it is outside the initial release. Artifact-only evidence works from an existing origin without reading its folder; judges receive an owned empty workspace if required by their adapter. There is no hidden dummy-repository requirement. ([control.ts:5541–5576][C17]; [claude.ts:1056–1067][C18])

Today a non-Git Council has no snapshot, its judges share the project folder, and cleanup skips snapshot removal. The thread folder must match the project folder so judges use the folder Sotto checked. File citations stay unchecked; Git snapshots are checked through Git objects. Climb preserves those ordinary-Council rules while adding source-specific capture/cleanup and manifest-backed validation. Folder/artifact capture must bypass Git entirely: the existing non-Git detector still invokes Git and is not sufficient when Git is absent. Text references preserve matches/differs/missing/unchecked; images identify frozen hash/page/region. Command claims require identified run receipts. ([councilSnapshot.ts:31–50][C01], [57–71][C02]; [councilService.ts:217–250][C14], [304–322][C35], [475–489][C15], [613–646][C03])

The preview discloses selected paths, exclusions, recipients, counts and limits. Proposed initial limits are 10,000 files, 100 MiB per evidence set, 20 MiB per binary and 1 GiB retained per Climb. They must be measured before release. Overflow pauses for narrowing or explicit bound revision; it never becomes a silent partial snapshot. Dependencies, caches, credentials and `.git` are excluded by default. Sensitive evidence is included only through explicit selection. Copy-on-write is an optional later optimization; writable links into another checkout are not acceptable isolation.

Compute changes from successive manifests, including additions, removals and binary replacements. Complete selected artifacts accompany changes. Keep the baseline available in every assessment. Export any retained round into a new folder, or restore supplied text as a draft, without overwriting live work. Recovery covers only captured scope. Automatic in-place rollback and forked builders are separate later features. Finishing retains evidence within the disclosed budget; settling/removing it explains loss of future export first.

The existing independent-worktree allocator still returns the original shared directory for non-Git projects before considering an existing worktree path. Ordinary Council intentionally asks for shared mode when there is no snapshot. Neither path isolates a copied Climb exhibit. A dedicated owned-evidence-workspace boundary remains required; copied folders must not pass through that fallback or silently return to the builder folder. `delegatedBy` remains only the origin-thread link, not a Git operation or command grant. Council needs its own ownership and Stop hooks because the existing Council link has no ordinary delegation record. ([threadWorktrees.ts:689–705][C16]; [councilService.ts:475–489][C15]; [ADR-0177:81–82][D02])

Judges must have enforced read-only tools/workspaces. The current narrowest-permission fallback is not that proof; ineligible adapters are refused for Climb judging until they can enforce it. ([councilService.ts:865–869][C31])

### Command judging changes the command-evidence rule

The current first-build wording that Sotto never runs Council command evidence, and the no-installed-dependencies snapshot limitation, continue to describe ordinary Council. ([ADR-0177:70][D12], [87][D02]) Climb may run a user-approved measurement recipe through a separate runner. A passing command is necessary only where the approved target says so and is never sufficient without panel integrity approval.

Commands run in disposable writable directories derived from frozen evidence, separate from judges, canonical evidence and the origin. The recipe fixes executable/arguments or exact shell script, machine, directory rule, environment/credential scope, dependency setup, metric parser and bounds. Its approval binds to its hash and permitted inputs. Changed commands, machine, setup, parser or authority scope require another approval.

**User decision — one bounded grant per Climb:** the user chose one explicit, bounded, expiring/revocable policy grant for repeated runs of the exact recipe within that Climb. Once the recipe is approved, the automatic loop may repeat it against permitted inputs within the grant's limits without asking before each run. Revocation, exhausted limits or a changed recipe pauses execution for a new approval. The chosen grant needs a companion ADR-0004 amendment implementing that specific allow consumer; current ADR-0004 says such consumers are future work. Without that amendment, do not implement unattended repeated command execution. This recorded product decision, the draft and any goal-approval text are not themselves command grants. ([ADR-0004:21–27,37–38][D07])

Windowless measurements may run locally if explicitly approved. Any app/browser/GUI launch or uncertain window behavior runs on **forge or CI, never this Mac**. Sotto's repository checks run on GitHub CI only. No-Git tasks use a hashed bundle and owned forge workspace, not a forced clone or GitHub PR. A missing remote transport or dependency is reported as unavailable; there is no local GUI fallback.

The existing check-worktree cache is Git/per-project/npm-specific and its install output is intentionally discarded. It is not the general runner or receipt store. ([checkWorktree.ts:8–15][C27], [39–54][C28], [103–126][C29])

A receipt binds metrics to input, recipe and environment identities with outcome, exit status and bounded outputs. Lost, malformed or truncated evidence cannot silently pass. Main evaluates the target from valid measurements only after the panel checks that inputs, requirements or evaluation scripts were not weakened. The panel sees raw values and relevant changes, not the target threshold. Noisy measurements also fix sampling, aggregation and tolerance, and compare baseline/reference measurements under the same declared environment.

### Records, migration, turns and retention

Version 2 of `councils.json` adds source-neutral evidence entries; per-round snapshot references and fresh thread rosters; approved specification revisions; raw round/seat/criterion/exhibit score cells; command receipts; loop state and owned-resource identities. Git metadata exists only on relevant variants. The public view changes with the stored shape. At the original audit, the file was version 1 with an **optional** singleton Git snapshot and neither stored nor public rounds had evidence references. The evidence foundation migrates that file to version 2; loop fields arrive with the following slice. ([councilRecord.ts:70–134][C04] [C05]; [shared/council.ts:336–379][C06])

Validate v1 before migrating it. For a present singleton, create one `legacyGit` entry referenced by all old rounds, preserving missing/removed status. For an absent singleton, retain a `legacyLive` descriptor: unfrozen evidence, no invented manifest or verified citations, and no owned source directory to remove. The v1 record has a project ID but no historical live-folder inventory; today's folder cannot reconstruct that history. Preserve turn IDs and unknown delivery in both paths. Active ordinary Councils retain their behavior; starting Climb captures a new approved baseline. Keep a migration backup and write the fully validated result atomically. Unknown future versions fail visibly without replacing history. This precedes normal corruption recovery, whose present behavior may back up a parse failure and return the default store. ([councilRecord.ts:113–134][C05]; [councilService.ts:228–250][C14], [304–322][C35]; [atomicJsonStore.ts:105–139][C26])

Keep local history applies to evidence and results as well as record prose. With it off, sensitive working evidence is temporary and cleared on close/settle or next-start cleanup; a restart cannot recover content that was not retained and must stop the loop clearly. After a history-off record is safely saved, the migration backup is removed so it cannot retain old Council text. Application logs never gain prompts, transcripts, outputs or keys. The current saved-record path strips words, and recovery stops an unfinished history-off Council whose question is gone. ([councilRecord.ts:160–173][C25]; [councilService.ts:133–153][C24])

Seats are admitted one at a time in the approved roster's order. Known prices are not used for internal ordering: incomplete or incomparable prices must not change a predictable user-approved order. Provider-limit holds do not remove seats or change denominators. Show completed and planned turns per round and for the run, including setup, builder, judges, adjudication and repairs. Climb has no spend cap and no estimated-cost display. Explanations live in tooltips that also open on keyboard focus; required decisions and blockers stay visible.

### Consequences and acceptance boundary

The first usable slice is panel-only Climb inside the current drawer with Git/folder/artifact evidence, setup approval, fresh judging, safe origin continuation, stop rules, migration and export. Basic non-Git startup, optional snapshots, no-snapshot cleanup, drawer placement, retained form state, reply handoff and From picker are already implemented and removed from Climb's build scope. Remaining capture/workspace/migration and loop/UI work are each estimated M–L, with a combined first slice L–XL; reuse reduces scope, while enforced read-only evidence and restart safety remain new cross-layer work. ([councilService.ts:217–250][C14], [304–322][C35], [475–489][C15]; [councilRecord.ts:113–134][C05]; [CouncilView.tsx:64–98,122–158,208–212][U02]; [councilDraft.ts:28–47][U03]) Command judging follows with authority and remote-execution work. Before/after callout boards, automatic rollback, forked builders and copy-on-write are deferred. Ordinary Council's framing, debate stages, live-folder rules and draft-only send are regression boundaries.

**User decision — Owl-only:** the user chose to keep Climb behind Council's current Owl gate. This amendment does not send Council or Climb to stable. The current Council feature entry has not been sent to stable. ([owlFeatures.ts:68][C33])

Implementation verification runs on GitHub CI, with deterministic provider/process fixtures and no-Git cases, plus forge/CI visual evidence where needed. No Sotto app, CI gates or graphical tests run on this Mac for this work. Windowless typecheck, lint and individual unit files are allowed. A controlled same-budget comparison and independent final assessment should determine whether Climb earns wider use. No claimed success rate, calibrated score threshold or speedup is accepted by this amendment.
