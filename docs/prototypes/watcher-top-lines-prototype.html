Critique, references and tradeoffs

What the screenshot shows

  • The second request starts at the column’s left edge; the first starts after the count. The two titles cannot be scanned down one line.
  • The lead and rings sit below the first title’s baseline. Underlines are the strongest repeating marks; the reasons have no fixed place.
  • Two 260px title allowances, their reasons and the lead exhaust the 760px column. Wrapping happens in the common two-request case.
  • The machine line introduces a third baseline, then a rule far longer than its text. Neither clarifies the relationship to the header or conversation.
  • The current narrow-window rule hides reasons. Small text over glyphs must be measured, and status must never depend on colour alone.

The choices

A · Open rows. A small count, one full-width row per request, reasons aligned at the end, machine activity underneath. Deletes the rule, permanent underlines, fixed title cap and the accidental wrapping layout. Keeps every reason visible. The spacing is the structure. No new surface or changed header.

B · One strip. The count opens the requests. Machine activity shares the line when there is room. Far less on screen at rest; one extra press to see a name. Changes the glossary’s promise that the line itself names the waiting threads. No pill, new tab, or status-shape exception.

C · Shared card. One filled card makes the two groups one piece. Requests on the left, machine activity on the right; narrow rooms stack them. Follows ADR-0110’s filled-card rule. Stronger structure, but adds more surface than A.

All keep five names before the remaining count. Full titles stay in accessible names and tooltips. Reasons and machine problems are words as well as colour. Names never get rewritten to guess what makes them different. Header, night scene, empty state and handover remain context. The dark screenshot colours are sampled; the light comparison uses Clay. The photographed glyph field is recoloured from each theme; this is not the running scene.

Scope of the prototype

This is a standalone layout study. It does not call Sotto or a provider. A pick is required before the real components change. The production build will retain request routing, keyboard handling, data-host anchors and the scene’s measured exclusions.

Rendered at 1600 × 1000, 1280 × 800 and 820 × 560 in light and dark. Long titles, seven requests, mixed reasons, four machines, no requests and a narrow room were checked. The measured top band clears the real glyph image. Text contrast meets 4.5:1 across all built-in themes; the lowest measured text ratio is 6.62:1.
