# Claude bot creature verification

October 22, 2026, on `feat/claude-bot-creature` from `main` at 03706b056. Drake Morey asked for a second creature above the composer, Claude bot, a switch for it in Settings → Appearance, and the readout's gray box gone, and left the bot's look to us. There was no prototype round: the choice is an affordance on a surface that already exists, and the character was ours to draw. The decision is ADR-0112; the glossary terms are **Creature**, **Claude bot** and the **readout** inside it.

## What changed

- `creature` is a setting (`sotto` or `claude`, Sotto by default), on the IPC allow-list, read by `useCreature()`. Settings → Appearance → **Creature** shows each character walking in a frame of its own, after Effort color. It is a plain setting, outside the appearance cache and preview, and every pane follows it at once.
- Claude bot is drawn as rows of letters in `claudeBotSprite.tsx` on the 40 × 32 grid Sotto's creature uses, one file per pose (`ClaudeBotMonitoring`, `ClaudeBotWorking`, `ClaudeBotHeld`, `ClaudeBotBabysitting`) over one shared sprite, `paintBot` and `useBotParts`. Its colours are the provider's token for Claude and its ink, shaded by mixing, so it follows the theme; babysitting mutes it as it mutes Sotto's creature. The shared frame loop and the hourglass geometry left `ThreadMonitor.tsx` for `pixelLoop.ts` and `hourglass.ts`; nothing about them changed, except that a window that cannot say whether motion is reduced (a test's) now holds one frame instead of looping.
- The readout has no fill and no outline (`.thread-monitor__task` in `threadMonitor.css`), for both creatures.

## Checks

- Sotto's creature is unchanged. Every pose, in both themes, on a 760 and a 340 px track, at nine times of the walk and in the reduced-motion stance, was rendered before and after (30 images, a virtual clock in a plain headless Chromium page that mounts the real components with the real theme tokens) and compared pixel by pixel with `main` carrying only the new readout rule: 0 pixels differ. Two runs of `main` against itself differ by a few pixels in the clipped column of the working pose, so that is the noise floor.
- Unit, `claudeBotSprite.test.tsx`: the picture's geometry, `pixelPath`, the gait (a trot, wrapping, a frame that is not a whole number), the legs even about the bot's centre, one stable set of parts, only the sprite's colour classes. The four pose files, `claudeBotMonitoring/Working/Held/Babysitting.test.tsx`: stance, schedule, reduced motion (the app's setting and the system's), the prop, the DOM contract the end-to-end specs measure. `claudeBotParity.test.tsx`: Claude bot's position, facing, minis and sand equal Sotto's creature's at sampled times, on two track widths and under both reduced-motion settings. The pose tests were checked by mutating copies of the poses: every mutant of walk time, speed, spacing, stance, mark and flip died. `creatureChoice.test.tsx` and the settings tests cover the choice and the setting's parse, default and schema.
- Electron, `tests/e2e/claude-bot-creature.spec.ts`: the choice saves and survives a reload and works from the keyboard (Enter and Space); every pose follows the setting and swaps in place when it changes; the readout has no fill, no outline, no shadow and no pseudo-element box, does not meet the transcript, and its text is at least 4.5:1 on the room in both themes at 1280 × 800 and 820 × 560; the bot fits every size in both themes, stays on its track with its feet on the composer's edge, and moves under motion and holds one stance under both reduced-motion settings. The existing `thread-monitoring`, `thread-held` and `babysitting` specs pass with their contrast checks now measured on the room instead of the readout's old fill. Text contrast on the room, from [readout-contrast.json](../../artifacts/claude-bot-creature/readout-contrast.json): label 19.55:1 and status 9.48:1 in dark, 15.74:1 and 6.72:1 in light.
- `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint` and `npm run notices:verify`: clean. `npm test -- --maxWorkers=2`, on the tree merged with `main` at 1588db304: 14,061 passed, 389 skipped, 4 failed in three files that are not this change's. `gitLauncher.test.ts` (two tests here, three on an earlier run) and `owlHost.test.mjs` (one) fail the same way on `main` at 03706b056 in a detached worktree; `threadWorktreeGitTimeout.test.ts` (one) is the known flaky file and passes when run alone.
- Review: a holistic review by the strongest model and three refute reviews (core, poses, UI with end-to-end and docs) found nothing blocking. What they found, all fixed: the legs were centred 1.5 units right of the body, so the bot's legs jumped three units when it turned; in the light theme the babysitting shading ran backwards (the shade paler than the body); `paintBot` threw on a non-integer step; the end-to-end contrast was measured against the composer's fill rather than the room under the readout; the "no box" check could not see a shadow or outline; and the pose tests let ten of fifteen mutations through. A full run of the unit suite then found the one real regression the reviews did not: the Settings tests that mount the Appearance page crashed in jsdom (`window.matchMedia is not a function`) once the preview walked, which the loop's new default fixed. Muse's adversarial review (the first attempt hit its time limit and counts for nothing) then raised four things, each checked by a separate reviewer: the two preview loops in Settings → Appearance ran for as long as Settings was open, whichever tab showed (the settings panels stay mounted), so a preview now mounts its creature only while it is on screen; the end-to-end geometry check measured the creature's fixed 80 × 64 box rather than what is painted, so a painted-bounds check now samples every shape the creature draws over a whole 14 s walk in both facings, a 3-agent working pose and a held glass through its flip, in both themes and at both sizes, and fails when the bot is pushed 12 units out of its box; the first painted frame of a Claude pose shows its unpainted stance before the loop's first frame, as Sotto's creature does (measured once in sixteen mounts, deferred); and the Windows design baselines are stale (disclosed below), so the four Appearance design captures now hold the previews still and are stable when regenerated.

## Not verified

- Windows and Linux: everything ran on an Apple silicon Mac. The creature is plain SVG and CSS, and Windows CI runs the gates.
- The design baselines of Settings → Appearance (`npm run design:capture`) were not regenerated: the page is one section taller and its baselines are Windows captures, so they need regenerating on a Windows machine. Design captures are not a CI gate (`docs/ci.md`).
- No live provider run: the poses were raised with scripted events, and babysitting only by unit tests and the component render above.
- No screen-reader pass: names and the group's label are asserted in the accessibility tree, not heard.
- The iPhone and Android clients draw no creature and were not touched.
