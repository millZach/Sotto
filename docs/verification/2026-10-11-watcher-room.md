# Watcher's room, October 11, 2026

Ticket 6 of [the Watcher plan](../plans/2026-10-08-command-center.md#ticket-6--add-the-third-room-and-sidebar-overview) added Watcher's room to Sotto Owl with a sidebar Overview. After seeing it, Zach decided the same day that the room has no sidebar, and picked from the [no-sidebar mock-up](C:/Users/zache/AppData/Local/zstack-scratch/watcher-room-no-sidebar/index.html), which stays outside the repository: the switch floats at the bottom centre (1C), All threads opens the Overview over the room (2D), a line under the header names what needs him (3A), the first entry is Watcher's composer (4C), a thread opens beside Watcher (5A), and the tile and sky keep the corner (6A). ADR-0073 and section 6 of the plan record the picks. This note says what the room now is, what I worked out where the mock-up did not reach, and how it was checked on the built app. The beside pane is ticket 7's "real beside pane"; ticket 7's receipt cards, takeover feedback and Stop all confirmation are not part of it.

## What an Owl build shows

- **The switch.** In every sidebar foot it reads Dictate, the owl, Threads. The owl is alone, 16 pixels, and its tooltip and accessible name are Watcher; it is in the accent colour while Watcher's room is open. Only the lit tab is in the Tab order, arrow keys, Home and End move between the rooms, and a press hands focus to the lit tab of the switch the opened page draws. Stable keeps Dictate | Threads.
- **No sidebar in Watcher's room.** The Sotto Owl tile keeps the window's top-left corner and the night sky spreads behind that corner of Watcher's header, fading before the conversation. Watcher's conversation is centred on the same 760-pixel column as a thread's.
- **The floating switch.** A pill centred in a 54-pixel band under the room: History, Settings and Help, the switch, the update control. The band is kept clear, so the pill never covers a composer.
- **Watcher's header.** Watcher's name, then **All threads** with the count of other threads, then, once Watcher runs, the meter (**2 of 4 watched**, **2 of 4** in a narrower pane) and **More Watcher actions**, which holds Threads in flight. The small owl that sat before the name is gone.
- **The needs-you line.** Under the header: "2 need you", then each name with its reason, **permission**, **question** or **last turn failed**. Each name opens the thread beside Watcher at what it waits on. Windows narrower than 980 pixels keep the names and drop the reasons; past five names the rest are counted, and the count opens All threads. With nothing waiting the line is not drawn.
- **The Overview over the room.** All threads drops a light list from its button: a filter, then Needs you, Ready for review, Working, Landing, Quiet and Idle with their counts, settled threads folded under Settled at the end of Idle. Each row has its ring, title, time, provider mark, Watcher's owl when watched, project and status, and a waiting thread's row says what it asks with **Open to answer** (**Open** for a failed turn). A press on a row opens the thread beside Watcher; Shift with it opens the thread in the Threads room.
- **A thread beside Watcher.** Its real pane, to the right of Watcher's: its own header with **Open in Threads**, More and Close, its request cards, transcript, composer and draft, and the watched note when Watcher watches it. A divider (**Resize panes**) shares the width; the focused pane carries the accent along its top edge. One thread at a time; opening another replaces it, and closing stops nothing.
- **The first time.** The room as it will be: header, needs-you line, a short account of Watcher, then Watcher's composer ready, with a model chip offering the user's Codex, Claude Code and Grok Build models (never Devin), the effort chip and **Ask before changes**. Under it: "Your first message starts Watcher on Sonnet 4.5, with your own Claude Code account." The send button is **Start Watcher and send**.
- **Unchanged from ticket 6.** Watcher's pane (composer labelled Watcher message, pinned asking mode, no Git, menu or branch controls, replies signed Watcher), Threads in flight, the badge on watched threads in the sidebar and pane headers, and the composer note on a watched thread.

Stable shows and reads none of it.

## What I worked out

- **The first message goes through the composer's own send.** The contract's `open` takes a provider, model and effort and no message, so the first send calls `open`, and once main names Watcher and its thread reaches the window, the message is put in Watcher's draft and sent with `sendThreadRevision`, as any composer sends. It shows in the transcript the same way. A refusal is said in main's words and the message stays in the composer. The first entry's message, model and effort are the window's, not the room's (`watcherFirstMessage.ts`), and the room's provider, which stays mounted when the room does not, sends the message: leaving the room while main creates Watcher keeps it, coming back shows it starting, and it goes to Watcher once, whether or not the room is open when Watcher's thread arrives. A reload or a closed window between the press and Watcher's thread arriving still loses it, since the draft store keeps drafts by thread and there is no thread until `open` answers; Watcher would exist and wait for its first message.
- **The first entry keeps the needs-you line and All threads.** Both work before Watcher exists, as the mock-up drew them; the meter and More Watcher actions wait for Watcher.
- **No tabs.** Without a sidebar the room is two 400-pixel panes plus the divider wide even at 820 by 560, so it never falls back to tabs; the divider keeps each pane at its minimum.
- **Focus.** A switch press lands on the floating switch's lit owl; arriving any other way with nothing focused puts focus in Watcher's composer. A thread opened from the needs-you line or Open to answer takes focus on its request card, the card itself rather than Allow or a choice inside it, so a stray Enter answers nothing. A thread waiting after a failed turn, opened at that turn, takes focus on the turn's line ("Failed after …", with the turn's error under it), brought into view; with no turn record to say where it failed, on its transcript, scrolled to the end, where that latest turn ended. Both wait for the thread's history, so a kept reading position put back when it arrives cannot move them. Opened any other way it takes focus on its title. Escape or Close returns focus to what opened it (All threads for a thread opened from the list), or to Watcher's composer when that is gone. Escape closes the beside pane only when nothing inside it (a menu, a picker, a docked card) answered it first, and never interrupts a turn. An open disclosure in a transcript (Worked for, an activity group or line, changed files) folds on Escape first, from its button or from inside what it shows, one level at a time with focus on its button (`foldOnEscape`, on the Threads page too); a control that is merely expanded, with no popup of its own, no longer keeps the pane from closing. More Watcher actions closes on Escape from its button as well as from its panel, since Shift+Tab back to the button leaves the panel open.
- **The floating switch's Tab order follows the eye left to right:** History, Settings, Help, the switch, the update control. The mock-up's notes, written for the foot bar, had the switch first; in the centred pill the page links come first.
- **The room's view lasts as long as the window.** The thread beside Watcher and the divider's position are kept when the user goes to another room and back, Watcher's reading position is kept for the return, and each beside thread's for its next opening (`useKeptReadingPosition`, one record per thread for both). The position is the message at the top of the view, not a pixel count, because a transcript drawn afresh can hold fewer messages than the one left; when that message is not drawn, the view comes back as far from the end. Nothing is put back or remembered until the thread's history is in the window: a thread the window's sixteen-thread history cache let go comes back as an empty transcript first, and that empty view is never kept as the reader's place. A reader back at the newest message follows it again. A restart starts the room with nothing beside Watcher and Watcher at its newest message.
- **Shift with a press, and Open in Threads,** select the thread on the Threads page and go there. These are the only ways the room changes the Threads page's selection, which the plan asks for.
- **A beside thread's pull request badge goes to Threads.** The room has no Tools panel, and fitting one beside two panes at 820 pixels would cost the panes their minimum, so the badge opens the Pull request surface in Tools and does what Open in Threads does: the Threads page, with the thread selected and Tools on its pull request. Its name says so: "Open PR #12 - Open: … in Tools on the Threads page". The badge reads where it goes from `PullRequestOpenerContext` in `BranchToolbar.tsx`; the Threads page keeps the default, Tools beside its panes.
- **A beside thread's links in Sotto's browser go to Threads too.** With Web links in threads set to Sotto's browser, or Open in Sotto browser from a link's menu, main opens the page in that thread's browser; in the room nothing showed it. The beside pane now takes the window to the Threads page with the thread selected and Tools on Browser at that page, pinned to the thread if Tools was pinned elsewhere (`BrowserPageRouteContext` in `tools/webLinks.tsx`, `showBrowserPageOf` in `toolsPanelStore.ts`). Watcher's own pane offers no Sotto browser at all: Watcher is not on the Threads page, so a page in its browser could never be seen, and its links open in the system browser.
- **Thread names in Watcher's replies do not open threads yet.** Nothing in the contract says which words in Watcher's prose name a thread, and the plan rules out parsing them; ticket 7's receipt cards carry that link. Written to the run's `room-needs.md`.
- **The Overview's foot says "Shift-click opens a thread in Threads. Esc closes."** The mock-up's foot said settled threads are in the Threads room; this room keeps them under Settled, as the plan asks, so the foot teaches the one gesture that is otherwise invisible.
- **Status colours lean toward the text.** The Overview's and the needs-you line's warning and success inks mix a fifth of the way toward the text colour, as ticket 6's Overview did, and the count on All threads lifts to the secondary text colour while the button is pressed: on its fill the muted count read 4.40:1 in Tropic dark.
- **Quiet, Landing, read marks and a check time** still wait for the projection to carry observations; the room-needs file lists them. In particular the Overview's rows show only their last activity time: the plan's **Read [time]**, and **Read by you**, **Read by Watcher** and **Observed [time]** told apart in each row's detail and accessible text, need read marks and observation times the room contract does not carry yet, so the rows say none of them rather than guess (room-needs items 1, 2 and 7).
- **macOS** gives the header a 64-pixel inset for the traffic lights and moves the sky with the tile. It is untested.

## Not proven here

- Most of Watcher's real main side. Since `main` was merged in with Watcher's thread tools (#930), an Owl build has the bridge: `src/preload/index.ts` exposes `window.sotto.watcher` and `src/main/index.ts` implements it with the Watcher service, so "This build cannot reach Watcher yet" shows only where the gate is on and no bridge is offered, and main itself refuses with its own words when the local host is off. Twelve journeys still use the stand-in, by design, to hold the room's picture still; the one journey against main proves its `open`, `setLimit` and the first message on Codex only.
- macOS and Linux, and scaling above 100%.
- Many threads beyond the fixture's nine on the built app. More than five threads waiting at once, where the needs-you line counts the rest, is covered by a unit test, not a run.
- A beside thread's kept reading position on the built app: the fixture's thread transcripts are too short to scroll, so it is covered by a unit test. Watcher's own is checked on the built app (the beside pane journey seeds a 40-message conversation, scrolls Watcher message 30 to the top, goes to Threads and back, and finds message 30 at the top again, though the transcript drawn afresh holds fewer messages).
- `npm run design:verify` checks stable only. Before `main` was merged in it passed against the committed baselines with none regenerated. Since the merge it reports five stable captures changed (`theme-hush-dark`, `onboarding-step-2-look`, `threads-search`, and `scale-125-onboarding` and `widget-idle-light` at another size), and `app.spec.ts`'s widget bounds test finds the widget 56 pixels tall where it expects 54. Both fail identically with `origin/main`'s own sources built in this worktree: main's #939 changed the built-in palettes and asked for the Windows baselines to be refreshed, which has not happened yet. None was regenerated here.
