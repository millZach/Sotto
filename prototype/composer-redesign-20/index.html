The prompt box: twenty directions

0 is the composer as it ships today, rebuilt from threads.css, branchToolbar.css and nextPrompt.css (ADR-0113 text buttons, ADR-0132 next prompt, ADR-0153 keys). 1 to 20 change its structure, not its colours: every frame takes its colour from the same theme roles, at the composer's real 760px width. Use the bar to show Dark, Light or both, and to switch between at rest, a next prompt waiting and a turn running, text typed. Hover 6 to open it.

What the critique of 0 found:

  • Six controls of nearly equal weight on one line (attach, model, fast, effort, permissions, Watch), then a second line of three more under the card. Nothing tells the eye which one matters; the model reads as the same grey as Watch.
  • Where the thread runs (computer, workspace, branch) sits outside the card it governs, so the card and its line read as two things, and the line costs a row of height.
  • Send with a word and a key cap is clear but wide; with a turn running the right side holds three buttons and nine characters of keys.
  • The next prompt hides in placeholder ink at 55%: at rest it is the most useful thing in the box and the faintest.
  • Strengths to keep: no boxes around options (ADR-0113), the one filled button is always what Enter does (ADR-0153), the trace on Stop says a turn runs without a word.

Against the references (Mobbin needs a paid plan, so: Claude Code desktop from Drake's screenshot; ChatGPT, Claude, Raycast, Locally AI and Microsoft Copilot from their App Store screenshots). Claude Code and ChatGPT win on quiet: one card, attach left, model and send right, nothing else visible. Raycast and Locally AI win on hierarchy by putting the model in a header above the input. Copilot's Mac panel wins on density with a one-line field. Sotto wins on honesty: none of them shows effort, permissions or the branch, and none teaches its keys. The directions below try to keep that honesty at their level of quiet.
